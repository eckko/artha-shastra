# Arthashastra (कौटिलीय अर्थशास्त्र)

- Author / compiler: Kauṭalya (Kauṭilya), Indian statecraft tradition
- Subject and purpose of the book: statecraft, administration, law, foreign policy and war; the text itself says it was composed by gathering the earlier teachers' arthaśāstras into one (1.1.1) and that it is easy to grasp and clear in meaning (1.1.19)
- Base edition: R. P. Kangle, *The Kauṭilīya Arthaśāstra*, Part I critical edition, Bombay 1960 (2nd ed. 1969), as typed by Muneo Tokunaga et al. (Kyoto 1993) and encoded by SARIT (source S1 in sources.md)
- Mapping: part = adhikaraṇa (book), chapter = adhyāya, verse = sūtra (prose) or śloka (verse) as numbered in the edition
- Totals: 15 parts, 150 chapters, 5398 verse numbers (5835 lines) in the draft count; traditional (text 1.1.18): 15 adhikaraṇas, 150 adhyāyas, 180 prakaraṇas, 6000 ślokas (the 6000 are ślokas of 32 syllables, so not comparable to the edition's sūtra numbers)
- Chapter names: the e-text carries only the editor's English headings (from Kangle's translation). The Sanskrit prakaraṇa list in 1.1.3-17 yields 175 names for 180 sections, so it cannot be mapped to chapters one-to-one; chapter Sanskrit names are left empty (—) rather than guessed.
