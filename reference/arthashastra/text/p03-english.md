03.01.1 | Three dharmasthas [and] three ministers should carry out legal (vyāvahārika) matters in the jana-pada, sandhi, saṅgrahaṇa, droṇa-mukha and sthānīya.
03.01.2 | They should also reject transactions (vyavahāra) made in concealment, inside the house, at night, in the forest, by upadhi, [or] in a secluded place.
03.01.3 | For the doer, for the one who has it done: the first fine for rashness.
03.01.4 | For the hearers, one by one, [there are] penalties of half each.
03.01.5 | But for the trustworthy, [there is] removal of property.
03.01.6 | Concealed [transactions]: the taking of an excess debt in absence, or [those] with an unspeakable kara, would be valid.
03.01.7 | [Transactions] concerned with inheritance, deposit, upanidhi [and] marriage, made inside the house, of women who do not go out and of the sick whose consciousness is unconfused, would be valid.
03.01.8 | [Transactions] concerned with rashness, entry, quarrel, marriage [and] royal appointment, and those made at night of persons who transact in the early part of the night, would be valid.
03.01.9 | In the midst of caravans, cattle-stations, hermitages, hunters [and] cāraṇas, [transactions] of forest-goers made in the forest would be valid.
03.01.10 | Among those with secret livelihoods, and [those] made by upadhi, [transactions] would be valid.
03.01.11 | In a mutual association, and [those] made in a secluded place, [transactions] would be valid.
03.01.12 | Otherwise than this they would not be valid, and [those] made by persons having a support, by a son who has a father, by a father who has a son, by a brother without family, by a younger [brother] with an undivided share, by a woman who has a husband and a son, by a slave and a pledged person, by one who has not reached and one who has passed beyond dealing (vyavahāra), and by the accused, renunciants, nyaṅga and persons given to vice (vyasana), except [for] those entrusted with transacting.
03.01.13 | Even there, transactions made by one angry, afflicted, intoxicated and mad, or seized would not be valid.
03.01.14 | For doers, those who have it done [and] hearers, the penalties [are] separately as stated.
03.01.15 | But all transactions made with their own means of proof, in each one's own class, place and time, complete in procedure, with a pure place, [and] with form, mark, measure and qualities observed, would be valid.
03.01.16 | And the last of these karaṇa [is] trustworthy, excluding ādeśa and pledge. — Such is the laying down of vyavahāra.
03.01.17 | Having written down the year, season, month, fortnight, day, karaṇa, adhikaraṇa, [and] debt, and the regions, villages, castes, gotras, names [and] actions of the vedaka and the āvedaka, [in their] done [and] capable states, he should enter the questions of plaintiff and defendant in the order of the matter.
03.01.18 | And he should examine the entered [ones].
03.01.19 | Having abandoned a fixed plea, he passes over to another plea; he does not join what was stated before with the later matter; having seized upon the other's statement that is not to be seized upon, he stands [by it]; having asserted [a claim] and being told “Specify the place”, he does not specify; he specifies a deficient place or a non-place; he brings forward a place other than the specified place; when the place is presented, he denies the statement of the matter [saying] “Not so”; he does not wish [to accept] what was ascertained by witnesses; in a place where talking is not allowed he converses privately with witnesses. — Such are the grounds of being parā-ukta.
03.01.20 | The penalty for the parā-ukta [is] of five bandhas.
03.01.21 | The penalty for the self-pleader [is] of ten bandhas.
03.01.22 | The wages of men [are] an eighth part.
03.01.23 | Food on the road [is] according to the difference of value.
03.01.24 | The one to be restrained should give both of those.
03.01.25 | The accused should not counter-charge, except [in cases of] quarrel, rashness [and] caravan-association.
03.01.26 | And in the case of the accused there is no charge.
03.01.27 | If the charger, when answered, does not reply on that very day, he would be parā-ukta.
03.01.28 | For the charger [is] one by whom the decision of the matter has been made, not the accused.
03.01.29 | For him who does not reply: “three nights, seven nights”.
03.01.30 | After this he should impose a penalty with three paṇas as the lower [limit] and twelve paṇas as the upper.
03.01.31 | After three fortnights, [in the case] of one who does not reply, having imposed the parā-ukta penalty, he should hand over to the charger, from whatever properties he may have, except the implements of livelihood.
03.01.32 | He should do that very [thing] for the accused who runs away.
03.01.33 | For the charger, the state of being parā-ukta [is] of the same time as the flight.
03.01.34 | The statement of a witness, [whether] of the deceased or of one given to vice (vyasana), [is] without substance.
03.01.35 | The charger, having paid the penalty, should have work done.
03.01.36 | Or he should have a pledge put in, at will.
03.01.37 | Or he should hand over, by work, [one] guarded by rakṣoghna, except [in the case of] a brāhmaṇa.
03.01.38a | Because of protecting the conduct of the world of the four varṇas and āśramas, this king [is] the promoter of dharma, all the dharmas perishing.

03.02.1 | The dealings (vyavahāra) are those having marriage first.
03.02.2 | The giving of a maiden, having adorned the maiden, is the Brāhma marriage.
03.02.3 | Practising dharma together is the Prājāpatya [marriage].
03.02.4 | From the taking of a pair of cattle [it is] the Ārṣa [marriage].
03.02.5 | From giving [the maiden] to an officiating priest within the altar [it is] the Daiva [marriage].
03.02.6 | From mutual union [it is] the Gāndharva [marriage].
03.02.7 | From the taking of bride-price [it is] the Āsura [marriage].
03.02.8 | From taking by force [it is] the Rākṣasa [marriage].
03.02.9 | From the taking of one asleep or intoxicated [it is] the Paiśāca [marriage].
03.02.10 | The first four, having the father as authority, are in accordance with dharma; the rest have mother and father as authority.
03.02.11 | For those two take the bride-price of the daughter, or, in the absence of one of the two, the other.
03.02.12 | The woman should take the second bride-price.
03.02.13 | For all, the placing (āropaṇam) through affection is not forbidden. Such is the dharma of marriage.
03.02.14 | Woman's property (strī-dhana) is maintenance (vṛtti) or ābandhya.
03.02.15 | Maintenance (vṛtti) is to be fixed at two thousand at the most; for ābandhya there is no restriction.
03.02.16 | There is no fault in the wife's enjoying that [property] in the maintenance of herself, [her] son and [her] daughter-in-law, and when no provision is made during a stay abroad; [likewise] of the husband, in the remedy of robbers, disease, famine and fear, and in a dharma-act; or jointly, of the couple who have produced a pair.
03.02.17 | And in the marriages most in accordance with dharma, one should not question [what has been] enjoyed for three years.
03.02.18 | As to what was enjoyed in the Gāndharva and Āsura [marriages], both [items] should be made to be given, with increase; what was enjoyed in the Rākṣasa and Paiśāca [marriages], [he] should give [it] as theft.
03.02.19 | When the husband is dead, [a woman] desiring dharma should receive at that very time the ornaments to be set aside and the remainder of the bride-price.
03.02.20 | Or, having received [them] and marrying, [she] should be made to give both, with increase.
03.02.21 | But [a woman] desiring a family should receive what was given by the father-in-law and the husband at the time of niveśa (settling).
03.02.22 | For we shall explain the time of niveśa (settling) in the case of a long stay abroad.
03.02.23 | Or [a woman] who has settled in opposition to the father-in-law should lose what was given by the father-in-law and the husband.
03.02.24 | For [a woman] laid hold of from the hand of relatives, the relatives should give [it] as it was taken.
03.02.25 | Of [a woman] who has come in accordance with rule, the one who takes charge [of her] should guard the woman's property.
03.02.26 | [A woman] marrying should lose the husband's share (dāya).
03.02.27 | [A woman] desiring dharma should enjoy [it].
03.02.28 | [A woman] having a son, marrying, should lose the woman's property.
03.02.29 | But the sons should take that woman's property.
03.02.30 | Or, marrying for the purpose of supporting [her] sons, she should make [it] increase for the sons' sake.
03.02.31 | For sons born of many men, [she] should settle the woman's property as given by the father.
03.02.32 | Even [the part of] woman's property that is to be done as desired, [a woman] marrying should make [it] established with the sons.
03.02.33 | A sonless [woman], guarding her husband's bed, should enjoy the woman's property near the elder until the waning of life.
03.02.34 | For woman's property has adversity as its purpose.
03.02.35 | [It] should go afterwards to the heir.
03.02.36 | While the husband is living, for one who has died, the sons and daughters should divide the woman's property; for a sonless [woman], the daughters; in their absence, the husband.
03.02.37 | The bride-price, the anvādheya, or another [gift] given by relatives: the kinsmen should take [it]. Such is the procedure (kalpa) concerning woman's property.
03.02.38 | [He] should wait eight years for a [woman] not giving birth, sonless and barren; [ten years] for a nindu; [twelve years] for one bearing daughters.
03.02.39 | Then, desiring a son, [he] should marry a second [wife].
03.02.40 | In the transgression of that, [he] should give the bride-price, the woman's property, and half [as] ādhivedanika, and a penalty of twenty-four paṇas at the most.
03.02.41 | Having given the bride-price and the woman's property to one without bride-price and woman's property, an ādhivedanika of that measure, and suitable maintenance, [he] may marry even many [women].
03.02.42 | For women have sons as their purpose.
03.02.43 | And when the tīrtha of these [women] coincide, [he] should go first, according to marriage, to the one married earlier or to one having a living son.
03.02.44 | In concealing the tīrtha and in approach, the penalty is ninety-six.
03.02.45 | [He] should not approach an unwilling [woman] who has a son, desires dharma, is barren, is a nindu, or is without rajas.
03.02.46 | And an unwilling man should not go to a [woman] afflicted with kuṣṭha (a skin affliction) or mad.
03.02.47 | But a woman should approach for the sake of a son, or [approach] one of such a kind.
03.02.48a | A husband who has gone forth to lowness or to another country, an offender against the king, one who strikes at life, fallen, or even impotent, is to be abandoned.

03.03.1 | A woman of twelve years becomes one who has reached dealings (vyavahāra); a man [is so] at sixteen years.
03.03.2 | After this, in disobedience, the penalty on a woman is twelve paṇas, on a man double. — Such is obedience (śuśrūṣā).
03.03.3 | In the case of a woman to be maintained, for whom no time is specified, [one] should give food and clothing, or more, with something extra, according to the person's parivāpa.
03.03.4 | In the case of [a woman] for whom the time is specified, having reckoned that very [thing], [one] should also give a bond (bandha).
03.03.5 | And in the non-taking of the śulka, the strī-dhana (woman's property) and the ādhivedanika.
03.03.6 | When [she] has entered the father-in-law's family, or is separated, the husband is not to be proceeded against. — Such is maintenance (bharma).
03.03.7 | “O lost one”, “O ruined one”, “O maimed one”, “O fatherless one”, “O motherless one” — without specifying [the fault], [there is] the making [her] take discipline.
03.03.8 | Or a striking on the back three times with any one of a split bamboo, a rope, [or] a hand.
03.03.9 | In the transgression of that, [there are] half-penalties, by the two penalties for verbal injury (vāk-pāruṣya) and physical injury (daṇḍa-pāruṣya).
03.03.10 | That very [penalty applies] to a woman, towards [her] husband, [in the case] of one whose fault is notorious.
03.03.11 | Out of jealousy, in outside amusements [and] at the doors, the penalty is as specified. — Such is harshness (pāruṣya).
03.03.12 | A woman hating [her] husband, not adorning [herself] for seven menstrual periods, at that very time, having laid down the ornaments to be deposited, should proceed against the husband who is lying with another [woman].
03.03.13 | Or the husband, hating [her], in any one of the female mendicant, the anvādhi, the kin [and] the family, should proceed against a single woman.
03.03.14 | When a sign is seen, in the carrying off [concerning] sexual union, or in approaching a spy of the same varṇa, one speaking falsely should pay twelve paṇas.
03.03.15 | A wife who hates [him] is not to be released when the husband does not desire [it]; and [likewise] the husband [who hates], when the wife [does not desire it].
03.03.16 | Release [comes] from mutual hatred.
03.03.17 | Or if the man desires release because of the woman's ill-treatment, [he] should give to her [it] as it was taken.
03.03.18 | Or if the woman desires release because of the man's ill-treatment, [one] should not give to her [it] as it was taken.
03.03.19 | There is no release of dharma marriages. — Such is hatred (dveṣa).
03.03.20 | A woman who has been forbidden, in pride, liquor [and] play, should pay a penalty of three paṇas.
03.03.21 | By day, in going to women's spectacles [and] amusements the penalty is six paṇas; in going to men's spectacles [and] amusements, twelve paṇas.
03.03.22 | At night, double.
03.03.23 | In the going out of [one who is] asleep [or] drunk, and in the non-giving of the door to the husband: twelve paṇas.
03.03.24 | In going out at night, double.
03.03.25 | Of woman and man, in movements of the limbs for the purpose of sexual union, or in indecent talk in secret: the penalty on the woman is twenty-four paṇas; on the man, double.
03.03.26 | In taking hold of the hair, the nīvi, the teeth [or] the nails, the first fine for rashness; on the man, double.
03.03.27 | In a suspected place and in conversation: in place of a paṇa, the śiphā penalty.
03.03.28 | The caṇḍāla should give women five śiphās in the middle of the village, at an interval of a pakṣa.
03.03.29 | Or [one] should cause a blow of a paṇa to be released. — Such is transgression (atīcāra).
03.03.30 | Of the forbidden woman and man, in mutual favour: the penalty is twelve paṇas for small things, twenty-four paṇas for coarse things, fifty-four paṇas for money and gold — the penalty on the woman; on the man, double.
03.03.31 | Those very [penalties] are half-penalties for the two who are not to be approached, and likewise in dealings with a forbidden man. — Such is prohibition (pratiṣedha).
03.03.32a | By [acts] hated by the king and by transgression, and by [her] own going away, non-ownership of the strī-dhana and of the śulka that has been brought arises for the woman.

03.04.1 | For a woman who has gone out from her husband's family, the penalty is six paṇas, except for ill-treatment.
03.04.2 | [In the case of a woman] who has been forbidden, [the penalty is] twelve paṇas.
03.04.3 | For [a woman] who has gone to a neighbour's house, [the penalty is] six paṇas.
03.04.4 | For giving room, alms and wares to neighbours, mendicants and traders, the penalty is twelve paṇas.
03.04.5 | For the forbidden [ones], [the penalty is] the first fine for rashness.
03.04.6 | For [a woman] who has gone to another's house, [the penalty is] twenty-four paṇas.
03.04.7 | For giving room to another's wife, [there is] a penalty of a hundred, except in adversities.
03.04.8 | In [cases of] prevention and ignorance, [the person is] blameless.
03.04.9 | “On account of the husband's ill-treatment, it is without fault to go to any one, [which is] without a man, of the families of the husband's kin, one in comfortable circumstances, a village headman, anvādhi, a woman mendicant, [and] kin,” say the teachers.
03.04.10 | Or the family of kin, with a man [in it].
03.04.11 | Whence, indeed, [could there be] deceit of virtuous women?
03.04.12 | “This is easy to understand,” says Kautilya.
03.04.13 | Going to the family of kin is indeed not forbidden for the sake of the deceased, disease, calamity or pregnancy.
03.04.14 | For one who prevents [it] on that account, the penalty is twelve paṇas.
03.04.15 | Even there, [a woman] concealing [it] would be deprived of [her] woman's property, or the kinsmen, concealing [it], [would be deprived of] the remainder of the śulka. Such is the going out (niṣpatana).
03.04.16 | Having gone out from the husband's family, on going to another village, the penalty is twelve paṇas and the loss of the sthāpya ornaments.
03.04.17 | Or, on setting out with an approachable man, [the penalty is] twenty-four paṇas and the loss of all dharma, except for giving maintenance and going to a tīrtha.
03.04.18 | For the man, [the penalty is] the first fine for rashness [in the case] of the equal and the better; for the worse, the middle.
03.04.19 | A relative is not to be punished.
03.04.20 | In [the case of] prohibition, [there are] half-penalties.
03.04.21 | On the road, in an interval, in going to a hidden place, for the purpose of sexual union, or in following on the road [a woman] who is suspected [and] forbidden, one should know [it to be] saṅgrahaṇa (literally 'seizing').
03.04.22 | For tāla-avacaras, cāraṇas, fishermen, hunters, cowherds, liquor-sellers and others whose women are set free, following on the road is without fault.
03.04.23 | Or, when [it is] forbidden, for a man who leads [her] or for a woman who goes, [there are] just those half-penalties. Such is the following on the road.
03.04.24 | The wives of śūdras, vaiśyas, kṣatriyas and brāhmaṇas who are away for a short time should await a time that is a year further, if they have not borne children; [a time] exceeding a year, if they have borne children.
03.04.25 | [A woman] who has been provided for [should await] twice the period.
03.04.26 | Those in comfortable circumstances should maintain those who have not been provided for; at most four years or eight, the kinsmen.
03.04.27 | Then, having taken [it] as given, they should release [her].
03.04.28 | [A woman] who has not borne children should, for ten years, await a brāhmaṇa who is studying; [one] who has borne children, for twelve [years]; [she should await] a royal servant until the end of [his] life.
03.04.29 | And [a woman] who has borne a child by [a man] of the same varṇa would not incur blame.
03.04.30 | Or, on the loss of the household's prosperity, [a woman] released by those in comfortable circumstances should find as desired; or one who has fallen into adversity for the sake of life.
03.04.31 | After a dharma marriage, a maiden should await for seven tīrthas the husband who has gone away without telling [her], not being heard of; [one who is] heard of, for a year.
03.04.32 | [She] should await for five tīrthas one who has gone away after telling [her], not being heard of; [one who is] heard of, for ten.
03.04.33 | [She] should await for three tīrthas [one] by whom the śulka was given in one part and who is not heard of; [one who is] heard of, for seven tīrthas.
03.04.34 | [She should await for] five tīrthas [one] who has given the śulka and is not heard of; [one who is] heard of, for ten.
03.04.35 | After that, released by the dharmasthas, she should find as desired.
03.04.36 | “Obstruction of the tīrtha is indeed destruction of dharma,” says Kautilya. Such is the short absence.
03.04.37 | The wife of one who is away for a long time, or who has gone forth, or of the deceased, should await seven tīrthas; [if] she has borne a child, a year.
03.04.38 | Then she should go to the husband's uterine brother.
03.04.39 | Among many, [to] the nearest, righteous [one], able to maintain, [to] the youngest, or one without a wife.
03.04.40 | Even in the absence of that [person], [she should go to] one not of the same womb, a sapiṇḍa, or a kinsman (kulya) who is near.
03.04.41 | Of these, this alone is the order.
03.04.42a | Having passed over these heirs, in obtaining [and] in the act of a paramour, the paramour, the woman, the giver and the obtainer have incurred the penalty for saṅgraha.

03.05.1 | Sons who have a father, whose father and mother are present, are not lords.
03.05.2 | After them [there is] the division of the inheritance from the father, of the father's property.
03.05.3 | What is self-acquired is not to be divided, except [in the case of] those risen from paternal property.
03.05.4 | Sons or grandsons of those who have come undivided from paternal property are sharers — “up to the fourth”.
03.05.5 | To that extent the piṇḍa is unbroken.
03.05.6 | Those whose piṇḍa is broken, all, should divide equally.
03.05.7 | Those who have no paternal property, or whose paternal property has been divided, living together, should divide again.
03.05.8 | And from whom a rise should come about, he should obtain a double share.
03.05.9 | The property of one without a son should be taken by uterine brothers, or by brothers living together, and by daughters.
03.05.10 | The inheritance of one having sons [should be taken by] sons or daughters born in the most righteous marriages.
03.05.11 | In their absence, the father, being alive.
03.05.12 | In the absence of the father, brothers and brothers' sons.
03.05.13 | Fatherless brothers and brothers' sons, even though many, should take one share of the father.
03.05.14 | Of those of the same womb who have several fathers, the division of inheritance is from the father.
03.05.15 | Among fathers, brothers and sons, when the earlier is present they do not depend on the later; and, the eldest [being present, they do not depend on] the youngest who takes the wealth.
03.05.16 | In a division while he is living, the father should not single out one.
03.05.17 | And he should not divide out one without cause.
03.05.18 | When the father's property does not exist, the elder ones should favour the younger, except [in the case of] those of false conduct.
03.05.19 | The division is of those who have reached dealings.
03.05.20 | Of those who have not reached dealings, [they] should place [the property], cleared of what is to be given, with the mother's kin or village elders until the reaching of dealings — or [that] of one who has gone away.
03.05.21 | They should give the naiveśanika to the unsettled, equal to the settled, and the prādānika to girls.
03.05.22 | Of debt and inheritance the division is equal.
03.05.23 | “Those who have nothing should divide even the water-vessels,” say the teachers.
03.05.24 | “This is a deceit,” says Kautilya.
03.05.25 | The division is of existing property, not of the non-existent.
03.05.26 | Having repeated “So much is the common property; of it, so much is each share,” and saying [it] among the witnesses, he should have the division made.
03.05.27 | What has been badly divided, taken from one another, concealed, or arisen without being known, they should divide again.
03.05.28 | The king should take the heirless [property], excluding the livelihood of women and the work for the deceased, except [in the case of] the property of a śrotriya (Veda-learned man).
03.05.29 | He should give that to those of the three Vedas (traivedya).
03.05.30 | The fallen one, one born of a fallen one, and the impotent are without shares; and the dull, mad, blind and leprous [are without shares].
03.05.31 | When there is the matter of a wife, their offspring, not of that kind, should take a share.
03.05.32 | Food and clothing — the others, the fallen being excluded.
03.05.33a | And of those who have taken wives, procreation being lost, the relatives should bring forth sons; he should fix their shares.

03.06.1 | Among sons of one wife, the eldest's share [is]: goats for brahmins, horses for kshatriyas, cows for vaishyas, sheep for shudras.
03.06.2 | Of them, the one-eyed and the lame [are] the middle share; those of different colour [are] the youngest share.
03.06.3 | In the absence of quadrupeds, the eldest should take one share, out of ten, of the goods other than jewels.
03.06.4 | For [he] becomes one with the noose of svadhā put on.
03.06.5 | Such is the Aushanasa division (that of the school of Ushanas).

03.18.1 | Verbal injury: defamation, disparagement, [and] threatening.
03.18.2 | Among [the defamations] of body, nature, learning, livelihood [and] country: in defamation of the body by [terms] such as 'one-eyed', 'lame' and the like, if true, the penalty [is] three paṇas; in false defamation, the penalty [is] six paṇas.
03.18.3 | "Having beautiful eyes" — in the praise-censure of the one-eyed, the lame and the like, the penalty [is] twelve paṇas.
03.18.4 | And in disparagement by [terms such as] leprosy, madness, impotence and the like, [and] in true [and] false praise [and] censure, penalties higher by twelve paṇas among equals.
03.18.5 | Among the superior, double [penalties]; among the inferior, half penalties; among others' wives, double [penalties]; by carelessness, intoxication, delusion and the like, half penalties.
03.18.6 | In leprosy and madness, physicians and men close by [are] the authority; in the state of impotence, women, urine-foam in water, and the sinking of faeces [are the authority].
03.18.7 | In defamation of nature, among Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Shudras [and] end-dwellers: [for defaming] the earlier by the later, penalties higher by three paṇas; [for defaming] the later by the earlier, [penalties] lower by two paṇas; and in disparagement by [terms such as] 'bad Brahmin' and the like.
03.18.8 | By that, the defamation of learning of those living by speech, the defamation of livelihood of artisans and kuśīlavas, and the defamations of country of the Prajjunakas, Gandharas and the like are explained.
03.18.9 | Whoever threatens another, "I shall make you thus," by doing [it] — in non-doing, [he] should give half of that penalty which [is] for the doing of that.
03.18.10 | [If one who is] powerless should allege anger, intoxication or delusion, [he] should give a penalty of twelve paṇas.
03.18.11 | [One] whose intent is arisen enmity and [who is] able to injure should give [a] life-long condition.
03.18.12a | For one's own region and village, the first; for caste and saṅgha, the middle; from reviling of gods and caityas [he] deserves the highest penalty.

03.19.1 | Physical injury: touching, threatening, striking.
03.19.2 | For one touching the body below the navel with hand, mud, ash [or] dust, the penalty [is] three paṇas; with those same impure [things] and with foot [and] spittle, six paṇas; with vomit, urine, excrement and the like, twelve paṇas.
03.19.3 | Above the navel double, on the head fourfold, among equals.
03.19.4 | Among the superior double, among the inferior half penalties, among others' wives double, through carelessness, intoxication, delusion and the like half penalties.
03.19.5 | In seizing by the foot, garment, hand [or] hair, penalties higher by six paṇas.
03.19.6 | In pressing, clasping, pulling, dragging [or] sitting upon, the first fine for rashness.
03.19.7 | For one who goes away after having caused [him] to fall, a half penalty.
03.19.8 | With whichever limb a śūdra strikes a brāhmaṇa, that [limb] of his [one] should have cut off.
03.19.9 | In threatening, redemption; in touching, a half penalty.
03.19.10 | By that, the caṇḍālas and the impure are explained.
03.19.11 | For threatening with the hand, the penalty [has] three paṇas as the lower [limit] and twelve paṇas as the upper; with the foot, double; with an article causing pain, the first fine for rashness; with [an article] endangering life, the middle.
03.19.12 | For one causing pain without blood by any one of the articles wood, clod, stone, iron, stick [or] rope, the penalty [is] twenty-four paṇas; in causing blood, double; except for bad blood.
03.19.13 | For one who strikes [a man] bloodlessly, like one dead, or who makes a pārañcika of hand and foot, the first fine for rashness; and in breaking a hand, foot [or] tooth, in cutting off an ear [or] nose, in splitting open a wound; except for bad wounds.
03.19.14 | In breaking the thigh [or] neck, or in splitting the eye, and in obstructing speech, movement [or] eating, the middle fine for rashness and the expense of rising up.
03.19.15 | In misfortune [he] should be led to the cleansing of thorns.
03.19.16 | Of a great crowd striking one, for each a double penalty.
03.19.17 | "A stale quarrel or an entering-in is not to be proceeded against," say the teachers.
03.19.18 | "There is no release for an offender," says Kautilya.
03.19.19 | "In a quarrel the one who came first wins, for the one who does not endure rushes forward," say the teachers.
03.19.20 | "No," says Kautilya.
03.19.21 | Of one who has approached first or afterwards, the witnesses [are] the authority; when there are no witnesses, the blow or the indication of the quarrel [is].
03.19.22 | Of one not replying to the charge of a blow, on that very day, the paścāt-kāra (literally "making-behind").
03.19.23 | In a quarrel, for one carrying off an article, a penalty of ten paṇas; in damaging a petty article, that and that much as penalty; in damaging a coarse article, that and a double penalty; in damaging a garment, ornament, money, gold [or] wares, that and the first fine for rashness.
03.19.24 | For one shaking another's wall by striking, a penalty of three paṇas; in cutting [and] splitting, six paṇas; and the remedy.
03.19.25 | For one throwing into another's house an article causing pain, a penalty of twelve paṇas; [an article] endangering life, the first fine for rashness.
03.19.26 | In causing pain to small animals with wood and the like, a penalty of a paṇa or double [that]; in causing blood, double.
03.19.27 | For large animals, in just these cases, a double penalty and the expense of rising up.
03.19.28 | Of trees of town [and] park that have flowers, fruit [and] shade: in cutting a shoot, six paṇas; in cutting a small branch, twelve paṇas; in cutting a stout branch, twenty-four paṇas; in destroying the trunk, the first fine for rashness; in cutting off entirely, the middle.
03.19.29 | In shrubs [and] creepers having flowers, fruit [and] shade, half penalties; and in the trees of holy places, penance groves [and] cremation grounds.
03.19.30a | In boundary trees, in caityas and in marked trees, and in the king's forests, those very penalties are to be made double.

03.20.1 | The superintendent of gambling should have gambling done one-mouthed.
03.20.2 | Elsewhere, for one who gambles, the penalty [is] of twelve paṇas, for the purpose of making known those who live in secret.
03.20.3 | “In a charge of gambling, for the winner the first fine for rashness, for the defeated the middle
03.20.4 | For this [one] [is] of a foolish kind, desirous of winning; he does not tolerate defeat,” say the teachers.
03.20.5 | “No,” says Kautilya.
03.20.6 | If the defeated one were made [one] having a double penalty, no one will approach the king.
03.20.7 | For gamblers [are] mostly false players.
03.20.8 | Their superintendents, pure, should place the kākaṇīs and the dice.
03.20.9 | In the placing of another of the kākaṇīs and dice, the penalty [is] of twelve paṇas; in false action, the first fine for rashness, the taking back of the won; and in upadhi, the penalty for theft.
03.20.10 | From the won property the superintendent should take five per hundred, the hire of the kākaṇī, dice, arālā and śalākā, and the buying of water, ground and work.
03.20.11 | [One] should make the pledge and the sale of the property.
03.20.12 | And in not preventing the faults of dice, ground and hand, the penalty [is] double.
03.20.13 | By that, the samāhvaya is explained, except the samāhvaya of knowledge and skill.
03.20.14 | But miscellaneous: in not giving, according to place and time, of [things] borrowed, hired, pledged and deposited; or in passing beyond place and time of the fixings of watch, shadow and sitting together; for one who makes a brāhmaṇa accomplish the gulmatara-deya; and in inviting above the prativeśa and anuveśa — the penalty [is] of twelve paṇas.
03.20.15 | For one not giving the thing directed; for one who oversteps a brother's wife with the hand; for one going to a courtesan held by another; for one buying wares that are to be spoken of by another; for one breaking open a sealed house; and for one practising the neighbour-forty-kulya harassment — the penalty [is] of forty-eight paṇas.
03.20.16 | In squandering, of the holder of the family capital; for one approaching by force a widow living at will; for a caṇḍāla touching an ārya woman; for one not running toward one near in adversity; for one doing a running toward without cause; for one feeding vṛṣala renunciants, beginning with Shakya and Ajivaka, in the tasks for gods and ancestors — the penalty [is] of a hundred.
03.20.17 | For one doing an unpermitted oath-statement questioning; and for an unappointed one [doing] the work of the appointed; for one injuring the manhood of small animals and bulls; and for one causing the embryo of a female slave to fall with medicine — the first fine for rashness.
03.20.18 | Of father and son, husband and wife, brother and sister, maternal uncle and sister's son, or pupil and teacher, for one abandoning one another [who is] not fallen; for one abandoning [him who has] gone forth with a caravan, or [abandoning him] in the middle of a village, the first fine for rashness; in a wilderness, the middle; for one causing bhreṣa for the purpose of that, the highest; among others setting out together, half penalties.
03.20.19 | For one binding, causing to bind a man not to be bound, or causing release of a bond; for one binding or causing to bind a child who has not reached dealings — the penalty [is] a thousand.
03.20.20 | By the difference of man and offence a difference of penalty is to be made.
03.20.21 | The tīrtha-kara, the ascetic, the sick, one wearied by hunger, thirst and the road, one of a country beyond, one afflicted by penalty, and the destitute are to be shown favour.
03.20.22 | The dharmasthas should do the matters of gods, brāhmaṇas, ascetics, women, children, the aged and the sick, of the helpless, of those not approaching; and they should not pass over [them] by the pretext of place, time and use.
03.20.23 | And men are to be honoured by reason of excess of knowledge, intellect, manliness, high birth and action.
03.20.24a | In this way the dharmasthas should do the matters, seeing without pretext, equal in all states, trustworthy, dear to the people.
