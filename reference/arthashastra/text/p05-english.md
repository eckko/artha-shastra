05.01.1 | The removal of thorns in the fort and the country has been stated.
05.01.2 | In the case of the king and the kingdom we shall state [it].
05.01.3 | As for those chiefs who live [off him] having restrained the king, or who are shared with the enemy: in their case the deployment of secret agents, or the support of the party of the seducible, [brings] success, as stated before; [or] secret instigation or a spy, as we shall state in the Pāragrāmika.
05.01.4 | But those chiefs who harm the kingdom, [being] favourites or united, and who cannot be restrained openly, [are] the treasonable; against them one who is fond of dharma should apply secret punishment.
05.01.5 | A secret agent, having incited the ill-treated brother of a treasonable high official, should present [him] to the king.
05.01.6 | The king should make him attack the treasonable one by granting [him] the enjoyment of the treasonable one's property.
05.01.7 | [He] should have the one who has attacked killed right there, by weapon or poison, [with the words]: "This is a brother-killer."
05.01.8 | By that, the pāraśava and the son of a female servant are explained.
05.01.9 | Or the brother, incited by a secret agent, should ask the treasonable high official for a share.
05.01.10 | The assassin who kills him—while he is sleeping at night at the door of the treasonable one's house, or staying elsewhere—should say: "This one, desirous of a share, is killed."
05.01.11 | Then, having supported the party of the slain [man], he should suppress the other.
05.01.12 | Or secret agents stationed near the treasonable one should threaten with killing the brother who is asking for a share.
05.01.13 | "Him at night"—[this] is the same [as before].
05.01.14 | Or of treasonable high officials: if a son approaches [the wives] of his father, or a father the wives of his son, or a brother [those] of a brother, the quarrel of those two, with a kāpaṭika at its head, is explained by the earlier [rule].
05.01.15 | A secret agent should instigate secretly the son of a treasonable high official, or one esteemed by himself: "You are a king's son; from fear of the enemy you have been placed here."
05.01.16 | The king should honour in private the one who has accepted [it]: "For you, for whom the time of heir-apparentship has come, I do not anoint [you] from fear of the high officials."
05.01.17 | The secret agent should engage him in the killing of the high officials.
05.01.18 | [He] should have the one who has attacked killed right there, [with the words]: "This is a father-killer."
05.01.19 | Or a female mendicant, having made the wife of the treasonable one live together [with her] by love-winning herbs, should outwit [her] with poison.
05.01.20 | — such is the application of āpya.
05.01.21 | [The king] should send the treasonable high official, with weak troops and accompanied by assassins, to strike down the forest tribes or an enemy village; or to install a country-warden or a frontier officer in a region separated by a wilderness; or to restrain the angered town-station; or to take [what is] to be conveyed across [with] a caravan; or, at the frontier, [what is] with what is to be taken back.
05.01.22 | When battle has begun, by night or by day, assassins or agents in the guise of robbers should kill [him]: "Killed in the attack."
05.01.23 | Or, [the king] having gone on a journey or a pleasure outing, should summon the treasonable high officials for an audience.
05.01.24 | They, having entered the middle enclosure together with assassins [carrying] concealed weapons, should give a search of themselves for the purpose of entering inside.
05.01.25 | Then the assassins, seized by the door-keepers, should say: "We have been employed by the treasonable ones."
05.01.26 | They, having made that known, should kill the treasonable ones.
05.01.27 | And in the place of the assassins others are to be killed.
05.01.28 | Or, [the king] having gone on a pleasure outing outside, should honour the treasonable ones whose dwellings are near.
05.01.29 | Or a bad woman in the guise of a queen should be caught in their dwellings at night—[this is] the same as the earlier [procedure].
05.01.30 | Or [an agent] should ask the treasonable high official for eatables and edibles, by praise: "Your cook or maker of eatables is excellent"; or, outside, somewhere, having gone on a road, [he should ask] for drink.
05.01.31 | Having mixed both of those with poison, at the tasting in return he should make just those two partake.
05.01.32 | Having made that known—"[They are] two poisoners"—he should have [them] killed.
05.01.33 | Or the agent in the guise of a holy man should make one given to abhicāra (hostile rites) take [this]: "By eating any one of the marked monitor lizard, tortoise, crab and kūṭa, you will obtain your wishes."
05.01.34 | [He] should have the one who has engaged in the action killed with poison or with iron pestles: "Killed by the failure of the action."
05.01.35 | Or an agent in the guise of a physician, having established a daurātmika (literally 'of evil nature') or incurable disease of the treasonable one, should outwit [him] with poison in the compounds of medicine and diet.
05.01.36 | Or agents posted in the guise of cooks and ārālikas should outwit the treasonable one with poison.
05.01.37 | — such is the restraint by the secret teaching (upaniṣad).
05.01.38 | But the restraint of the treasonable ones of both [kinds] —
05.01.39 | Where a treasonable one is to be restrained, there he should send just the treasonable one, accompanied by weak troops and assassins: "Go; in that fort or country raise an army, or money; or fetch money from the favourite; or, having seized by force, bring the favourite's daughter; or have any one of the works of forts, embankments, trade routes, settlement of empty land, mines, and forests of materials and of elephants done, or the guarding of the country or of the frontier. And whoever should hinder you, or should not give you help, he is to be bound."
05.01.40 | Just so, for the others he should send [them]: "The misconduct of so-and-so is to be restrained."
05.01.41 | The assassins, having dropped a weapon, should kill him covertly while he quarrels in these occasions of quarrel or in the obstructions of works.
05.01.42 | By that fault the others are to be controlled.
05.01.43 | When a quarrel has arisen among the treasonable ones of towns, villages or families—over the boundary-limits of border, field, threshing-floor and house, in injury to goods, implements, crops and vehicles, and at shows, functions and festivals—or has been produced by assassins, the assassins, having dropped a weapon, should say: "Thus are they treated who quarrel with this one."
05.01.44 | By that fault the others are to be controlled.
05.01.45 | Or, of those treasonable ones whose quarrels have taken root, the assassins, having set fire to their fields, threshing-floors and houses, and having dropped a weapon among kinsmen and relations or in vehicles, should say in just the same way: "We have been employed by this one."
05.01.46 | By that fault the others are to be controlled.
05.01.47 | Or secret agents should have the treasonable ones of the fort and of the country made āveśanikas of each other.
05.01.48 | There the poisoners should give poison.
05.01.49 | By that fault the others are to be controlled.
05.01.50 | Or a female mendicant should instigate secretly a treasonable chief of the country: "The wife, daughter-in-law or daughter of a treasonable chief of the country desires [you]."
05.01.51 | Having taken an ornament of the one who has accepted, [she] should show it to the master: "That chief of yours, elated with youth, has his mind on a wife, daughter-in-law or daughter."
05.01.52 | The quarrel of those two: "at night"—[this] is the same [as before].
05.01.53 | But among the treasonable ones who have submitted with their army—the heir-apparent or the commander of the army, having done some harm and gone away, may attack.
05.01.54 | Then the king should send just those treasonable ones who have submitted with their army, accompanied by weak troops and assassins—all the contrivances are the same.
05.01.55 | And among their sons who are anukṣiyat [obscure], the one who is unchanged should obtain his father's inheritance.
05.01.56 | Thus his kingdom, with the fault of men cast away, follows his sons and grandsons.
05.01.57a | A forbearing, unsuspected [king] should silently apply punishment against his own party or the enemy's party, both in the future and in the present.

05.02.1 | One who has no treasury and is in difficulty over a present need should gather a treasury.
05.02.2 | He should ask a country that is large, or of small extent, not dependent on rain and with abundant grain, for a third or a fourth share of the grain, according to quality, middling or lower.
05.02.3 | He should not ask [for a share] from a frontier [country] that is useful for work on forts and embankments, trade routes, the settling of empty [land], mines, and work in the forests of materials and elephants, or from [one] of small extent.
05.02.4 | He should give grain, cattle, gold and the like to one who is settling.
05.02.5 | He should also buy with gold a fourth share of the grains, clear of seed and food.
05.02.6 | He should spare what is born in the forest and the property of a śrotriya (one learned in the Veda).
05.02.7 | Even that he should buy as a favour.
05.02.8 | Or, if that is not done, the Collector's men should have the udvāpa of the farmers carried out in summer.
05.02.9 | Declaring the penalty (atyaya) of one who has lapsed through negligence to be double, they should make a seed record at seed-time.
05.02.10 | When [the crop] is ready, they should prevent the taking of [what is] green and ripe, except for śāka-kaṭa-bhaṅga and a fistful (muṣṭi), for the purpose of worship of gods and ancestors and of giving, or for the purpose of cows.
05.02.11 | And they should spare the base of the heap for the sake of mendicants and village hirelings.
05.02.12 | For one who carries off his own crop, the pratipāta [is] eightfold.
05.02.13 | For one who carries off another's crop, the sītā-atyaya [is] fiftyfold, for one of his own group; but for an outsider [it is] death.
05.02.14 | They should take a fourth share of grains and a sixth of forest products — of wares of cotton-down, lac, linen, bark, cotton, rauma, silk, medicinal herbs, perfumes, flowers, fruit and vegetables, and of timber, bamboo, meat and dried meat — and half of tooth and hide.
05.02.15 | For one who sells that when it has not been released, [there is] the first fine for rashness (sāhasa).
05.02.16 | Such is the request (praṇaya) among farmers.
05.02.17 | Traders in gold, silver, diamonds, gems, pearls, coral, horses and elephants [pay] a tax of fifty.
05.02.18 | Traders in thread, cloth, copper, vṛtta, bell-metal, perfumes, medicines and śīdhu liquor [pay] a tax of forty.
05.02.19 | Traders in grain, juices and iron, and those who do business with carts, [pay] a tax of thirty.
05.02.20 | Those who do business in glass and the major craftsmen [pay] a tax of twenty.
05.02.21 | Minor craftsmen and those who maintain bandhakīs (unchaste women) [pay] a tax of ten.
05.02.22 | Traders in wood, bamboo, stone, earthen vessels, cooked food and green [produce] [pay] a tax of five.
05.02.23 | Actors and courtesans should give half of their wages.
05.02.24 | They should have those fit for work bring a tax in gold, and they should not let off any offence of these.
05.02.25 | For, having brought forward what has not been taken up (aparigṛhīta), they might sell [it].
05.02.26 | Such is the request (praṇaya) among traders.
05.02.27 | [As for] fowls and pigs, he should give half; small livestock [should give] a sixth part; and cows, buffaloes, mules, donkeys and camels a tenth part.
05.02.28 | Those who maintain bandhakīs should gather the treasury through the king's maidservants of the highest beauty and youth.
05.02.29 | Such is the request (praṇaya) among those who maintain yoni.
05.02.30 | [It] is to be applied only once, not twice.
05.02.31 | Or, if that is not done, the Collector, alleging a task, should beg of the citizens and country people.
05.02.32 | And the secret agents should give here first, excessively.
05.02.33 | By this indication the king should beg of the citizens and country people.
05.02.34 | And the kāpaṭikas (literally 'deceitful ones') should revile these [people] when they give little.
05.02.35 | Or he should ask gold of the wealthy according to [their] substance, or according to the benefit, or whatever they may offer of their own accord.
05.02.36 | And for money he should give these [persons] positions, parasols, turbans and ornaments.
05.02.37 | The task-doers should present the property of a pāṣaṇḍa saṅgha, or property to be enjoyed by a non-śrotriya (one not learned in the Veda), or a deity's property, as "deposited in the hand of a dead man or of one whose house is burnt".
05.02.38 | The superintendent of deities should make a treasury of the deities of the fort and of the country, each according to its own, kept in one place, and just in the same way should present [it].
05.02.39 | Having set up at night a caitya of a deity, a holy place of a holy man, or an aupapādika one, he should make a living by processions and gatherings.
05.02.40 | Or by a tree of a caitya grove endowed with out-of-season flowers and fruits he should make known the coming of the deity.
05.02.41 | Or, having displayed a human hand and fear of a rakṣas on a tree, agents in the guise of holy men should counteract [it] for the townsmen and country people with money.
05.02.42 | Or, in a well fitted with a tunnel, he should show a serpent with an unrestrained head, with an offering of money.
05.02.43 | In an image of a nāga hidden within, or in a hole of a caitya or in a hole of an anthill, having made [it] one whose signal is bound by food, he should show the sight of a serpent to the faithful.
05.02.44 | Having caused poison to be administered in the sippings and sprinklings of the unbelieving, he should declare the curse of the deity; or, having caused a condemned man to be bitten.
05.02.45 | Or he should bring about the gathering of the treasury by the remedy of the showing of the contrivance.
05.02.46 | Or an agent in the guise of a trader, with abundant wares and apprentices, should carry on business.
05.02.47 | When he has become increased in the value of wares by deposits and loans, then he should have him robbed at night.
05.02.48 | By this the examiner of coins and the goldsmith have been explained.
05.02.49 | Or an agent in the guise of a trader, of well-known dealings, should take, for the purpose of prahavaṇa, many articles of silver and gold, borrowed or hired.
05.02.50 | Or at a gathering, by a collection of all wares, he should take abundant money and gold as a loan, and the price of the pratibhāṇḍa.
05.02.51 | He should have both of those robbed at night.
05.02.52 | Having infatuated the treasonable through women in the guise of virtuous women, and having seized [them] in the houses of those very women, they should carry off all their possessions.
05.02.53 | Or, when a dispute has arisen among the kinsmen of the treasonable, poisoners who have been deployed should give poison.
05.02.54 | By that fault the others are to be seized.
05.02.55 | Or a condemned man should demand from the treasonable one, [on] a credible pretext, merchandise, a deposit of money, a loan placed out, or an inheritance.
05.02.56 | Or he should lay hold of the treasonable one by the word "slave"; his wife, daughter-in-law or daughter — by the word "slave-woman" or by the word "wife".
05.02.57 | Having killed him — lying at night at the door of the treasonable one's house, or dwelling elsewhere — the assassin should say: "This wealth-desiring one has been killed."
05.02.58 | By that fault the others are to be seized.
05.02.59 | Or an agent in the guise of a holy man, having tempted the treasonable one with jambhaka sciences, should say: "I know the action — inexhaustible money, relating to the king's gate, women's hearts, making disease in enemies, life-promoting, or son-related."
05.02.60 | He should have the one who has accepted make an offering at the caitya place at night, with abundant liquor, meat and scent.
05.02.61 | And here, where money of a single form, buried earlier, a limb of a corpse or a dead child may have been placed, from there he should show him money and should say: "Very little."
05.02.62 | "'For the sake of abundant money the offering must be made again': just yourself, with this money, buy abundant offering-material tomorrow."
05.02.63 | He would be seized with that money at the purchase of the offering-material.
05.02.64 | Or a female agent in the guise of a mother should be enraged: "My son has been killed by you."
05.02.65 | When his night-sacrifice, forest-sacrifice or forest-sport has begun — [it being] just accomplished — the assassins, having slaughtered the condemned man, should carry [him] across.
05.02.66 | Or an agent in the guise of a hired servant of the treasonable one, having thrown a false coin into the wage-money, should display [it].
05.02.67 | Or an agent in the guise of a labourer, doing work in the house, should place implements of thieves and makers of false coin; or an agent in the guise of a physician — gara, under the pretext of medicine.
05.02.68 | Or a secret agent close to the treasonable one should report, through a kāpaṭika, the consecration-vessels sent and the enemy's order, and should state the reason.
05.02.69 | Thus he should proceed with the treasonable and the unrighteous, not with the others.
05.02.70a | Like ripe [fruit] after ripe [fruit] from a garden, he should obtain fruit from the kingdom; from fear of cutting off himself, he should avoid the unripe, which causes anger.

05.03.1 | By the power of fort and country he should fix the servants' work (karma) at a quarter of the revenue, or by the acquisition of servants capable of accomplishing the work.
05.03.2 | He should look to the body; he should not harm dharma and wealth (artha).
05.03.3 | The officiating priest, teacher, minister, chaplain, commander of the army, heir-apparent, king's mother and king's chief queens are [those] of forty-eight thousand.
05.03.4 | With this much maintenance there comes to be in them anāspadyatva (the state of having no footing) and freedom from anger.
05.03.5 | The dauvārika (door-keeper), antarvaṃśika, praśāstṛ, Collector and Treasurer are [those] of twenty-four thousand.
05.03.6 | With this much they become fit for work.
05.03.7 | The prince, the prince's mother, the nāyaka, the townsman, the vyāvahārika, the kārmāntika, the council of ministers and the frontier officers of the country are [those] of twelve thousand.
05.03.8 | For with this much they become the king's paribandha, army and helpers.
05.03.9 | The chiefs of the guilds, the chiefs of elephants, horses and chariots, and the pradeṣṭṛs are [those] of eight thousand.
05.03.10 | For with this much they become those who draw their own group along with them.
05.03.11 | The superintendents of foot-soldiers, horses, chariots and elephants, and the guards of the dravya forests and elephant forests, are [those] of four thousand.
05.03.12 | The rathikas, the anīkasthas, the physicians, the horse-trainers, the carpenters and the yoni-poṣakas are [those] of two thousand.
05.03.13 | The fortune-tellers, omen-readers, astrologers, Purāṇa-reciters, bards and minstrels, the chaplain's men and all the superintendents are [those] of one thousand.
05.03.14 | The skilled men, the foot-soldiers, and the group of accountants, scribes and the like are [those] of five hundred.
05.03.15 | The kuśīlavas, however, are [those] of two and a half hundred; and of these the tūrya-karas are of double pay.
05.03.16 | The artisans and craftsmen are [those] of twenty hundreds.
05.03.17 | The attendants of quadrupeds and bipeds, the pārikarmikas, the aupasthāyikas, the keepers and the binders of forced labour (viṣṭi) are of sixty pay; the āryas, yuktas, ārohakas, māṇavakas, the diggers of rock, and all the attendants —
05.03.18 | The teachers and the learned should receive honorary pay (pūjā-vetana) according to merit, five hundred at the least and a thousand at the most.
05.03.19 | The envoy of the middle rank [has] ten paṇas for a yojana; beyond ten [yojanas] he is of double pay, up to a hundred yojanas.
05.03.20 | For [those] of equal learning, the king [is] of triple pay in sacrifices such as the Rājasūya.
05.03.21 | The king's charioteer is [a man] of one thousand.
05.03.22 | The agents in the guise of kāpaṭikas, udāsthitas, householders, traders and ascetics are [those] of one thousand.
05.03.23 | The village hirelings, secret agents, assassins, poisoners and female mendicants are [those] of five hundred.
05.03.24 | The spies (cāra) and wanderers are [those] of two and a half hundred, or have pay increased by exertion.
05.03.25 | The superintendents of the groups of a hundred and the groups of a thousand should make the receipt of food and pay, the assignment (ādeśa) and the dispersal (vikṣepa).
05.03.26 | And no dispersal in the supervision and protection of the king's household (parigraha), the fort and the country.
05.03.27 | There should be permanent chiefs and several chiefs.
05.03.28 | The sons and wives of those who have died in work (karma) should receive food and pay.
05.03.29 | Of these, the young, the old and the sick are to be favoured.
05.03.30 | And in the matters concerning the dead, the sick and women in childbirth, he should do for them the action of wealth and honour.
05.03.31 | One with a small treasury should give raw materials, cattle and fields, and a little money.

05.03.33 | By this he should make a difference in food and pay for the maintained and the unmaintained, also according to learning and work (karma).
05.03.34 | Having made an āḍhaka for [one] of sixty pay, he should make the food in proportion to the money.
05.03.35 | Foot-soldiers, horses, chariots and elephants should perform exercises in skill (śilpa) at sunrise outside, excepting the junction days.
05.03.36 | In these the king should be constantly engaged, and he should often view their skill.
05.03.37 | He should cause the weapons and armour, marked with the king's sign, to enter the armoury.
05.03.38 | They should move about unarmed, except [what is] permitted by a pass (mudrā).
05.03.39 | [If it is] lost or destroyed, he should give double.
05.03.40 | And he should make a count of the destroyed [things].
05.03.41 | The frontier officers should take the weapons and armour of the caravan-members, or cause [them] to go down to the sea.
05.03.42 | Or, having set out on a march, he should set the army in motion.
05.03.43 | Then the agents in the guise of traders should give all wares to the soldiers (āyudhīya) at the time of the march, to be taken back at double.

05.03.45 | Thus one who has examined income and expenditure does not incur calamity of the treasury and the army.

05.04.1 | One who knows the ways of the world should resort to a king endowed with self-possession and the material constituent elements, by way of what is agreeable and beneficial.
05.04.2 | Or [the king] of whom he may think: "Just as I desire shelter, so this one desires discipline and is endowed with approachable qualities" — even one lacking the material constituent elements he should resort to, but certainly not one who is not endowed with self-possession.
05.04.3 | For one who is not self-possessed, through hatred of the treatise on policy or through association with the worthless, even having obtained great sovereignty, does not come to be.
05.04.4 | In the case of a self-possessed [king], having found an opportunity, he should give instruction in the treatise.
05.04.5 | For by consistency he obtains steadiness of position.
05.04.6 | Asked in matters of counsel, he should say, for the present and for the future, [what is] connected with dharma and wealth, [and] capable, like a skilled man, not afraid of the assembly.
05.04.7 | Desired [by the king], he should stipulate: "You should not [do] this: the instruction in dharma and wealth, the wielding of punishment among the undistinguished [and] among those allied with the powerful, [and] the wielding of punishment in association with me and in the present; you should not injure my side, livelihood or secret; and by a sign I would restrain you in matters of desire, anger and punishing."
05.04.8 | Directed [and] permitted, he should enter on the ground assigned, and should sit at the side, [neither] near [nor] far, on another's seat.
05.04.9 | He should not make contentious speaking, [or] indecent, not-evident, unbelievable and false speech, [or] loud laughter at what is no jest, [or] noisy wind and spitting.
05.04.10 | He should avoid: speaking privately with another; speaking in pairs in public talk; the dress of the king and of the arrogant and of tricksters; requesting a display of the excess of jewels; twisting one eye and the lip, knitting the brows, and casting aside speech at one who speaks; opposition to one allied with the powerful; association with women, with those who look at women, with envoys of neighbouring chiefs, with those cast out by the hated party, and with petitioners; going about for one purpose; and the crowd.
05.04.11a | Without missing the time, he should speak of the king's interest, of his own interest together with the agreeable and beneficial, and of another's interest, in place and time, [what is] connected with dharma and wealth.

05.05.5 | And he should observe his hints and outward appearance.
05.05.6 | For the wise man, for the sake of concealing counsel, practises by hints and outward appearance the reversal of desire, hatred, joy, dejection, resolve, fear and the pairs.
05.05.7 | On being seen he is pleased; he accepts [his] speech; he gives a seat; in seclusion he shows [himself]; in a place of suspicion he is not over-suspicious; he delights in conversation; in matters to be made known he looks [to them]; he bears what is said that is wholesome; smiling he appoints [him]; he touches [him] with the hand; he does not mock at what is praiseworthy; he speaks of [his] merit in [his] absence; he remembers [him] in eatables; he goes together [with him] to amusement; in calamity he comes to [his] aid; he honours those devoted to him; he tells [him] a secret; he increases [his] honour; he makes wealth; he wards off harm — such is the knowledge of the satisfied.
05.05.8 | This very thing, reversed, [is that] of the dissatisfied, and we shall say more.
05.05.9 | On meeting, anger; non-hearing and rejection of speech; not giving a seat or an eye; change of colour and voice; twisting of one eye, brow and lip; the arising out of place of sweat, breath and smiles; consultation with another; going off suddenly; advancement of another; scratching the ground and the body; prodding of another; disparagement of learning, varṇa and region; censure of an equal fault; censure of a counter-fault; praise of the adverse; disregard of good deeds; recounting of bad deeds; attention to the back; excessive giving up; false addressing; and, of those who see the king, a difference in that conduct.
05.05.10 | And he should observe even the change of behaviour of non-humans.
05.05.11 | “This one sprinkles on high,” [said] Katyayana, and went forth; “The krauñca [bird] — anticlockwise,” says Kaninka Bharadvaja; “Grass,” says Dirgha Charayana; “A cold cloth,” says Ghotamukha; “The elephant sprinkled back,” says Kinjalka; “He praised the chariot-horse,” says Pishuna; at the answering cry of a dog, the son of Pishuna.
05.05.12 | And abandonment in the case of slighting of wealth and honour.
05.05.13 | Or, having perceived the master's character and his own fault, he should counteract.
05.05.14 | Or he should go to his friend, one drawn near.
05.05.15a | Staying there, he should bring about the removal of the fault through friends and with the master. Then, the master being alive or dead, he should return again.

05.06.1 | In this way the minister should remedy the calamity of the king.
05.06.2 | Even beforehand, from fear of the king's death or affliction, with the support of [his] dear ones and well-wishers, [the minister] should arrange a viewing [of the king] at an interval of a month or two months, on the pretext: “The king is accomplishing an action that removes the country's affliction, that removes enemies, and that is conducive to long life or to sons.”
05.06.3 | At a formless hour he should show the king's insignia to the constituent elements, and to the envoys of allies and enemies.
05.06.4 | And with them, [he], having the minister at the head, should enter into fitting conversation.
05.06.5 | And, with the doorkeeper and the antarvaṃśika at the head, he should keep up the king's routine as stated.
05.06.6 | Toward wrongdoers he should show anger or favour liked by the constituent elements; toward benefactors, favour only.
05.06.7 | He should have the treasury and the army, placed under trusted men and situated in a fort or on the frontier, stationed together; and [he should have] the kinsmen, princes and chiefs [so placed] on another pretext.
05.06.8 | And whichever chief, having a following and staying in a fort or a forest, may fall into disaffection, him he should have won over.
05.06.9 | Or he should send [him] on an expedition full of many hardships, or [to] the family of an ally.
05.06.10 | And the neighbour from whom he sees trouble, him he should have restrained on the pretext of a festival, a wedding, the tying of elephants, the sale of horses or a gift of land, or through his own ally.
05.06.11 | Then he should have an unobjectionable treaty made.
05.06.12 | Or he should make [him] take up enmity with forest chiefs and enemies.
05.06.13 | Or he should have a member of his family, or a prince in disfavour, won over with one part of the land.
05.06.14 | Or, having secured the support of the kinsmen, princes and chiefs, he should show the prince as consecrated indeed.
05.06.15 | Or, like a dāṇḍakarmika (one engaged in punitive action), having uprooted the thorns of the kingdom, he should have the kingdom ruled.
05.06.16 | Or if some chief, one of the neighbours and the rest, should resort to anger, he should call him — “Come, I shall make you king” — and have him killed.
05.06.17 | Or he should deal with [it] by the remedy for adversity.
05.06.18 | Or, having gradually placed the burden of the kingdom on the heir-apparent, he should announce the calamity of the king.
05.06.19 | When the king's calamity occurs in an enemy's land, having had a treaty with the enemy established by an ally in the guise of an enemy, he should withdraw.
05.06.20 | Or, having placed one of the neighbours and the rest in his fort, he should withdraw.
05.06.21 | Or, having consecrated the prince, he should draw up a counter-array.
05.06.22 | Or, when attacked by an enemy, he should apply the remedy for adversity as stated.
05.06.23 | “In this way the minister should bring about sole sovereignty,” says Kautilya.
05.06.24 | “Not so,” says Bharadvaja.
05.06.25 | “Or, when the king is dying, the minister should make the kinsmen, princes and chiefs attack one another, or [attack] the chiefs.
05.06.26 | He should have the one who has attacked killed by an uprising of the constituent elements.
05.06.27 | Or, having dealt with the kinsmen, princes and chiefs by secret punishment, he should take the kingdom himself.
05.06.28 | For on account of the kingdom a father plots against [his] sons and sons against [their] father; how much more then the minister element, which indeed has sole control of the kingdom?
05.06.29 | That, come of itself, he should not disregard.
05.06.30 | “A woman who has come up of her own accord curses when abandoned,” is the popular saying.
05.06.31a | And the time comes once to the man who desires the time; that time is hard to get again for him who wishes to do the action.

05.06.32 | “This causes an uprising of the constituent elements, is most unrighteous and uncertain,” says Kautilya.
05.06.33 | He should install in the kingdom a king's son endowed with [excellence of] self.
05.06.34 | In the absence of one so endowed, having put forward a prince free from vice, or a king's daughter, or a pregnant queen, and having assembled the high officials, he should say: “This is a deposit for you; look to his father, [his] spirit and high birth, and your own; this one is only a banner, you, sirs, alone are the masters; or how is it to be done?”
05.06.35 | To him who speaks thus the secret agents should say: “Who other than this king, with you at his head, is fit to protect the four varṇas?”
05.06.36 | [Saying] “So be it,” the minister should put the prince, or the king's daughter, or the pregnant queen in authority, and should show [him or her] to kinsmen and relatives and to the envoys of allies and enemies.
05.06.37 | He should have a distinction made in rations and wages for the ministers and the men who live by arms; “And this one, when grown up, will do more,” he should say.
05.06.38 | In the same way he should address the chiefs of the fort and the country, and, as is fitting, the party of allies and enemies.
05.06.39 | And he should strive in the disciplining of the prince.
05.06.40 | Or, having had offspring produced on the daughter from one of equal birth, he should consecrate [it].
05.06.41 | From fear of agitation of the mother's mind, he should place near [her] a kinsman of little spirit and a pupil bearing auspicious marks.
05.06.42 | And in the [fertile] season he should guard her.
05.06.43 | And he should not have any superior enjoyment provided for himself.
05.06.44 | But for the king's sake he should have vehicles, mounts, ornaments, clothes, women, houses and parivāpa provided.
05.06.45a | And he should ask the one who is in youth for rest, on account of the mind. He should abandon the one who is not satisfied, and look after the one who is satisfied.
