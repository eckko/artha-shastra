13.01.1 | The conqueror, desiring to obtain an enemy's village, should elate his own party by proclaiming [his] omniscience and [his] association with deities, and should alarm the enemy's party.
13.01.2 | The proclamation of omniscience, however, [consists in]: through knowledge of the secret doings in houses, the pointing out of the chiefs; through knowledge [obtained] from the spies of the removal of thorns, the exposure of those who do what the king dislikes; the proclamation of the present to be reported, by unseen contact, lore, signals and the like; and knowledge of events abroad on that very day, by a house-pigeon furnished with a seal.
13.01.3 | The proclamation of association with deities, however, [consists in]: conversation and worship by [agents] in the guise of fire, shrine and deity who have entered, through the mouth of a tunnel, the hollows of the images of the fire, shrine and deity; or conversation and worship by [agents] risen out of the water in the guise of Nagas and Varuna; at night, having placed a sea-sand receptacle inside the water, the sight of a garland of fire; staying on a raft held by a stone and a sling; or, with the head [covered] by a water-bladder or by afterbirth-membrane and the nose concealed, by the fats of the spotted deer, intestine, crab, crocodile, porpoise and otter, or the application through the nose of oil cooked a hundred times.
13.01.4 | By that, the night-troop moves about.
13.01.5 | Thus [are] the water-goings.
13.01.6 | By them, the making of speeches of Varuna and the Naga maidens, and conversation; on occasions of anger, the emission of fire and smoke from the mouth.
13.01.7 | In his own country, fortune-tellers, omen-readers, astrologers, Purana-reciters, observers, secret agents, those who render assistance, and those who have seen that, should make known that [about] him.
13.01.8 | In the enemy's country, they should tell of his sight of deities and of the arising of a divine treasury and army.
13.01.9 | In questions to deities, omens, crows, the lore of limbs, dreams, and the cries of beasts and birds, they should tell of his victory, [they should tell] the reverse of the enemy's.
13.01.10 | And they should show a meteor accompanied by a drum in the enemy's nakṣatra (lunar mansion).
13.01.11 | Agents in the guise of envoys, pointing out the enemy's chiefs as allies, should tell of the king's honouring [of them], the bestowal of strength on his own party and the repulse of the enemy's party.
13.01.12 | They should relate the equal acquisition and security of the ministers and of the men of arms.
13.01.13 | Towards them, he should apply attention to [their] calamity and prosperity and the honouring of [their] children.
13.01.14 | By that, he should rouse the enemy's party, as said before.
13.01.15 | And we shall say more.
13.01.16 | The skilled, by a common ass; the staff-goers, by striking with club and branch; and the agitated, by a family ram; the dishonoured, by a rain of thunderbolts; those whose hopes are shattered, by vidula, avakeśin, a crow's ball and a cloud born of deceit, thus; those whose fruit is honour, by an ornament of the unfortunate and by a hostile one, thus; the placed, by a tiger-skin and a death-trap; the always helpful, by the eating of pīlu, by karaka-yoṣṭrā and by the churning of she-ass's milk, thus; thus.
13.01.17 | He should provide those who have accepted with wealth and honour.
13.01.18 | And in the gaps of goods and food, he should favour them with gifts of goods and food.
13.01.19 | To those who do not accept, they should bring ornaments of women and boys.

13.05.3 | Having obtained a new acquisition, he should cover over the enemy's faults with his own virtues, and [the enemy's] virtues with a doubling of virtues.
13.05.4 | And by acts of favour for one's own dharma and action, of exemption, of gift and of honour, he should follow what is dear and beneficial to the constituent elements.
13.05.5 | And he should win over the party of the seducible as was discussed, and further [one] who has made effort.
13.05.6 | For distrust becomes a cause of discord for his own people and for others, and [so does] conduct contrary to the constituent elements.
13.05.7 | Therefore he should attain likeness in character, dress, speech and conduct.
13.05.8 | And in the deities of the region, in gatherings, festivals and amusements, he should keep up devotion.
13.05.9 | And among the chiefs of regions, villages, castes and saṅghas, secret agents should constantly show the enemy's misconduct, and the king's greatness and devotion towards them, and the king's honouring [of them] that exists.
13.05.10 | And with fitting enjoyments, exemptions, protection and supervision he should enjoy these.
13.05.11 | He should have worship of all deities and hermitages [done], and have gifts of land and wealth and exemptions [granted] to men of learning, speech, dharma and valour; [he should have] release from all bonds [done] and favour to the poor, the helpless and the sick.
13.05.12 | At the Cāturmāsyas, non-slaughter lasting half a month; on full-moon days, [lasting] four nights; on the nakṣatras of the king and the country, [lasting] one night.
13.05.13 | He should prohibit the killing of the womb and of the young, and injury to virility.
13.05.14 | And whatever practice he considers harmful to the treasury and army, or most unrighteous, that he should remove, and establish a procedure in conformity with dharma.
13.05.15 | He should have a change of place, located in several places, [made] for those of thievish nature and for mleccha tribes, and also for the chiefs of the fort, the country and the army.
13.05.16 | And for ministers and chaplains supported by the enemy, he should have a residence made in several places in the enemy's frontier regions.
13.05.17 | He should pacify by secret punishment those capable of harm, or the anukṣiyataḥ (obscure), on the destruction of [their] master.
13.05.18 | He should settle those of his own country, or those shut out by the enemy, in places that have been cleared.
13.05.19 | And whoever is a kinsman of his family able to take back what is to be taken back, or, being well-born and stationed in a frontier forest, able to harass — to him he should give land lacking good qualities, or a fourth part of [land] having good qualities, after fixing the giving of treasury and army, whereby, doing a favour, he would anger the citizens and country people.
13.05.20 | With them angered, he should have him killed by them.
13.05.21 | One who is denounced by the constituent elements he should remove, or he should settle [him] in a region liable to destruction.
13.05.22 | In the previous [case], that fault of the constituent elements by which [it/he] had turned away he should cover over, and that virtue by which [it/he] had come back he should intensify.
13.05.23 | In the ancestral [case], he should cover over the father's faults and make [his] virtues known.
13.05.24a | He should set going practice that is in conformity with dharma, [both] what has not been done [before] and what has been done by others; he should not set going [practice] not in conformity with dharma, and he should stop what has been done by others [of that kind].
