04.01.1 | Three pradeṣṭṛs and three ministers should carry out the cleansing of thorns (kaṇṭaka-śodhana).
04.01.2 | The arthya-pratīkāras, the controllers of artisans, the sannikṣeptṛs, the artisans of their own wealth, [and those] having the guild as authority should take the deposit.
04.01.3 | In misfortune the guild should take on the deposit.
04.01.4 | And they should do work whose place, time and task are specified; [for work] whose place and time are not specified, [there is] the designation of the task.
04.01.5 | On exceeding the time, the pay [is] less by a quarter, and the fine [is] twice that.
04.01.6 | Except in the case of bhreṣa and upanipāta, they should make good what is lost or destroyed.
04.01.7 | In doing the work otherwise, [there is] loss of pay and a fine twice that.
04.01.8 | The weavers should increase the ten-eleven (daśa-ekādaśika) yarn.
04.01.9 | In cutting off the increase, the fine [is] twice the cut.
04.01.10 | The price of the yarn [is] the pay for weaving; for linen (kṣauma) and silk (kauśeya), one and a half times; for pattra-ūrṇā, blanket (kambala) and dukūla, twofold.
04.01.11 | In deficient measure, the pay [is] lessened by the shortfall and the fine [is] twice that; in deficient weight, the fine [is] four times the deficiency; in exchanging the yarn, [the fine is] twice the price.
04.01.12 | By that, the weaving of double cloth is explained.
04.01.13 | For a tulā of wool, [there is] a five-pala beating-cut (vihanana-cheda) and a cut of hair (roma-cheda).
04.01.14 | Washermen should wash cloths on wooden boards and smooth stones.
04.01.15 | Those washing elsewhere should give [for] the damage to the cloth and a fine of six paṇas.
04.01.16 | Those wearing cloth other than [that with] the mallet mark should give a fine of three paṇas.
04.01.17 | In sale, hire and pledge of another's cloth, the fine [is] twelve paṇas; in exchange, [the fine is] twice the price, and the giving of cloth.
04.01.18 | They should give [them] — mukula-avadāta, clean on a stone slab, of the colour of washed yarn, and wiped white — one night later.
04.01.19 | The five-night, lightly dyed; the six-night, blue; and the seven-night cloth — dyed with flowers, lac and mañjiṣṭhā, of heavy treatment, to be treated with care, of fine kind.
04.01.20 | After that, they should incur loss of pay.
04.01.21 | In disputes about dyeing, trustworthy experts should fix the pay.
04.01.22 | For the most valuable, the pay [is] a paṇa; for the middling, half a paṇa; for the lowest, a quarter; for the coarse, a māṣaka, two māṣakas; for the dyed, twofold.
04.01.23 | In the first washing, the loss [is] a fourth part; in the second, a fifth part.
04.01.24 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.25 | The tailors are explained by the washermen.
04.01.26 | Of goldsmiths who buy silver [or] gold from an impure hand without declaring [it]: [if it is] of the same form, the fine [is] twelve paṇas; [if] of altered form, twenty-four paṇas; [if bought] from a thief's hand, forty-eight paṇas.
04.01.27 | In purchases [that are] concealed, of altered form, [or] short of the price, the fine for theft [applies]; and in deceit about made wares.
04.01.28 | For one who takes away a māṣaka from a suvarṇa, the fine [is] two hundred; for one who takes away a māṣaka from a silver dharaṇa, twelve paṇas.
04.01.29 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.30 | For one who effects raising of colour, removal, or a combination, the fine [is] five hundred.
04.01.31 | In the misconduct of those two, he should know [it as] the taking away of the colour.
04.01.32 | The pay [is] a māṣaka for a silver dharaṇa, [and] an eighth part for a suvarṇa.
04.01.33 | By special training, the increase of pay [is] twofold.
04.01.34 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.35 | Of copper, vṛtta, bell-metal, vaikṛntaka, āra and kūṭaka, the pay is five in the hundred.
04.01.36 | A lump of copper [has] a loss of a tenth part.
04.01.37 | When [it is] deficient in palas, the fine is twice the deficiency.
04.01.38 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.39 | A lump of lead and tin [has] a loss of a twentieth part.
04.01.40 | And a kākaṇī is the pay for a pala of this.
04.01.41 | A lump of black iron [has] a loss of a fifth part.
04.01.42 | And two kākaṇīs are the pay for a pala of this.
04.01.43 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.44 | For the coin-examiner who disturbs the established paṇa-yātrā that is not to be disturbed, [or] does not disturb one that is to be disturbed, the fine is twelve paṇas.
04.01.45 | On the clearing of the vyājī, [it is] the paṇa-yātrā.
04.01.46 | For one who lives on a māṣaka [out of] the paṇas, the fine is twelve paṇas.
04.01.47 | By that, what follows is explained.
04.01.48 | For one who has a false coin made, accepts it, or sends it out, the fine is a thousand; for one who throws it into the treasury, death.
04.01.49 | Wanderers and dust-washers: a third part of the valuables; the king: two [parts], and the gem.
04.01.50 | For theft of a gem, the highest fine.
04.01.51 | In reporting mines, gems and treasure-troves, the informant should get a sixth share, the hired man a twelfth share.
04.01.52 | A treasure-trove above a hundred thousand goes to the king.
04.01.53 | If [it is] less, he should give a sixth share.
04.01.54 | A pure country person should get in full a treasure-trove of former generations, by his own means of proof.
04.01.55 | In the absence of his own means of proof, the fine is five hundred; in secret taking, a thousand.
04.01.56 | For a physician who undertakes [a case] endangering life without informing [anyone], in a mishap the first fine for rashness; in a mishap through a fault in the action, the middle one.
04.01.57 | In causing injury to a vital spot, death or impairment, one should know [it] as physical injury (daṇḍa-pāruṣya).
04.01.58 | The kuśīlavas should dwell, staying in one place, during the rainy season.
04.01.59 | They should avoid excessive giving at will and the over-speaking of one.
04.01.60 | In transgression of that, the fine is twelve paṇas.
04.01.61 | As they like, they may jest by ridiculing region, caste, gotra, caraṇa and sexual intercourse.
04.01.62 | By [what is said of] the kuśīlavas, the cāraṇas and mendicants are explained.
04.01.63 | For them, as many paṇas as they declare by an iron spike, so many strokes of the śiphā are the fines.
04.01.64 | Of the remaining works, he should fix the craftsmen's completion-pay.
04.01.65a | In this way he should restrain thieves, [and] those called non-thieves, merchants, artisans, kuśīlavas, mendicants, kuhakas and others, from affliction of the country.

04.02.1 | The superintendent of the saṃsthā should establish, in the paṇya-saṃsthā (the establishment for wares), the pledge or sale of old goods that are cleared by their own means of proof.
04.02.2 | And he should inspect the balances, measures and vessels, on account of malpractice by the pautava.
04.02.3 | In the parimāṇī and the droṇa, a deficiency or an excess of half a pala is no fault.
04.02.4 | In a deficiency or excess of a pala, the fine is twelve paṇas.
04.02.5 | By this, the increase of the fine that rises by a pala is explained.
04.02.6 | In a balance, a deficiency or an excess of a karṣa is no fault.
04.02.7 | In a deficiency or excess of two karṣas, the fine is six paṇas.
04.02.8 | By this, the increase of the fine that rises by a karṣa is explained.
04.02.9 | For an āḍhaka, a deficiency or an excess of half a karṣa is no fault.
04.02.10 | In a deficiency or excess of a karṣa, the fine is three paṇas.
04.02.11 | By this, the increase of the fine that rises by a karṣa is explained.
04.02.12 | For the kinds of balances and measures other than these, he should make an inference.
04.02.13 | For one who buys with an excessive balance and measure and sells with deficient ones, those very fines are doubled.
04.02.14 | In the case of countable wares, for one who takes away an eighth part from the prices of wares, the fine is ninety-six.
04.02.15 | For one who puts into sale or pledge an article that is not genuine — made of wood, iron or jewels, or made of rope, leather or clay, or made of thread, bark or hair — as “genuine”, the fine is eight times the price.
04.02.16 | For one who puts into sale or pledge goods without substance as “goods of substance”, goods not born of that as “born of that”, goods endowed with upadhi as “endowed with rādhā”, or casket-exchanged goods (samudga-parivartima): for [goods] of lower price the fine is fifty-four paṇas; for [goods] priced at one paṇa, [the fine is] double; for [goods] priced at two paṇas, [the fine is] two hundred.
04.02.17 | By this, in a rise of price, the increase of the fine is explained.
04.02.18 | For artisans and craftsmen who, joining together, bring about a lowering of the quality of work, or livelihood (ājīva), or an obstruction of sale and purchase, the fine is a thousand.
04.02.19 | Or for traders who, joining together, hold back wares, or who sell at an anargha [price], the fine is a thousand.
04.02.20 | For a dharaka or a māyaka who practises, by a fault of the hand, a difference of balance and measure, or a difference of price and colour, [amounting to] an eighth part of the price of a paṇa, the fine is two hundred.
04.02.21 | By this, the increase of the fine that rises by two hundred is explained.
04.02.22 | In the adding of a like-coloured [substance] to articles of grain, oil, sugar, salt, fragrant things and medicinal things, the fine is twelve paṇas.
04.02.23 | What has been allotted, on which they may live — that of theirs which arises in a day, a merchant should fix after reckoning.
04.02.24 | What falls between the buyer and the seller is other than the ādāya.
04.02.25 | And by this, those who are permitted may make stores of grain and wares.
04.02.26 | What of these has been stored otherwise [than so], the Superintendent of Wares should take.
04.02.27 | By this, in the sale of grain and wares, he should deal with favour to the people.
04.02.28 | And beyond the permitted purchase, for these wares of one's own country he should fix the livelihood (ājīva) at five per hundred; for those of another country, at ten [per hundred].
04.02.29 | Beyond that, for those who raise the price, or bring [it] about, in purchase or sale, the fine is two hundred, [counted] from five paṇas in a hundred paṇas.
04.02.30 | By this, in a rise of price, the increase of the fine is explained.
04.02.31 | And in a joint purchase of these, [while it is] unsold, he should not give another joint purchase.
04.02.32 | And in damage to the wares of these, he should show favour.
04.02.33 | Because of the abundance of wares, the Superintendent of Wares should sell all the wares one-mouthed (eka-mukha).
04.02.34 | While those are unsold, others should not sell.
04.02.35 | They should sell those for a day's wage, with favour to the people. But of wares separated by place and time —
04.02.36a | Having reckoned the addition (prakṣepa), the production of the wares, the toll, the increase, the hire (avakraya) and the other expenditures, one who knows prices should fix the price.

04.03.1 | The divine [ones] are eight great fears: fire, water, disease, famine, rats, beasts of prey, snakes and demons.
04.03.2 | [He] should protect the country from them.
04.03.3 | In summer the villages should make an outside hearth, or [be] presided over with a collection of daśamūlī.
04.03.4 | The prevention of fire is explained in the nāgarika-praṇidhi, in the niśānta-praṇidhi and in the rāja-parigraha.
04.03.5 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of fire performed with offerings, oblations and benedictions.
04.03.6 | During the rainy season the villages of watery land should dwell, having left the flood-shore.
04.03.7 | And [they] should collect timber, bamboo and boats.
04.03.8 | They should cause [one] being carried away to cross with gourds, skin-bags, floats, gaṇḍikās and veṇikās.
04.03.9 | For those who do not go toward [them], the fine is twelve paṇas, except for those without floats.
04.03.10 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of rivers performed.
04.03.11 | Those who know illusion-contrivance (māyā-yoga) or those who know the Veda should perform abhicāra upon the rain.
04.03.12 | When rain is withheld, [he] should have worship performed of the Lord of Shachi, the Ganga, the mountain and the Mahakaccha.
04.03.13 | [They] should counteract the fear of disease with aupaniṣadika remedies; physicians [should do so] with medicines, or holy men and ascetics with pacifications and expiations.
04.03.14 | By that, pestilence is explained.
04.03.15 | [He] should have performed the sprinkling at holy fords, the increase of the Mahakaccha, the cremation-ground milking of cows, the burning of a headless trunk, and the night of the gods.
04.03.16 | In a pestilence of animal disease, [he] should have performed the lustration (nīrājana) for the sake of the place, and the worship of [their] own deity.
04.03.17 | In famine the king, having made provision of seed and food, should do a favour; or the work on forts and embankments through the favour of food; or a sharing out of food; or depositing in a region.
04.03.18 | Or [he] should take refuge with allies, or do weakening or vomiting.
04.03.19 | Or [he], together with the country, should go to another territory with ripened crops, or should resort to the sea, lakes and tanks.
04.03.20 | Or [he] should make sowings of grain, vegetables, roots and fruits on the embankments, or undertakings of game, cattle, birds, beasts of prey and fish.
04.03.21 | In fear of rats: the release of cats and mongooses.
04.03.22 | In the capture and injury of them the fine is twelve paṇas, and in the non-restraint of dogs, except for forest-roamers.
04.03.23 | [He] should put out grains smeared with snuhi milk, or joined with upaniṣad-contrivance.
04.03.24 | Or [he] should employ the mūṣika-kara.
04.03.25 | Or holy men and ascetics should perform a pacification.
04.03.26 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of rats performed.
04.03.27 | By that, the remedies for fear of locusts, birds and worms are explained.
04.03.28 | In fear of beasts of prey, [he] should send out animal carcasses joined with madana poison, or audarya [things] filled with madana and kodrava.
04.03.29 | Hunters or those with packs of dogs should go about with traps, cages and pitfalls.
04.03.30 | Those with a covering [and] weapons in hand should strike the beasts of prey.
04.03.31 | The fine of one who does not go toward [them] is twelve paṇas.
04.03.32 | That same is the gain of the killer of beasts of prey.
04.03.33 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of the mountain performed.
04.03.34 | By that, the remedies for the seizing of herds of game, cattle and birds are explained.
04.03.35 | In fear of snakes, those who know jāṅgulī should go about with mantras and herbs.
04.03.36 | Or, having come together, they should even kill the snakes.
04.03.37 | Or those who know the Atharvaveda should perform abhicāra.
04.03.38 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of Nagas performed.
04.03.39 | By that, the remedies for fear of creatures of the water are explained.
04.03.40 | In fear of demons, those who know the Atharvaveda or those who know illusion-contrivance (māyā-yoga) should perform demon-destroying actions.
04.03.41 | And on festival days [he] should have worship of caityas performed with platforms, parasols, ullopikās, hand-banners and goat-offerings.
04.03.42 | In all fears they should perform, day and night, in this manner: “We perform a caru for you.”
04.03.43 | And everywhere [he] should favour the afflicted like a father.
04.03.44a | Therefore, in the territory, those who know illusion-contrivance (māyā-yoga), holy men and ascetics should dwell, honoured by the king, counteracting adversity from the divine.

04.04.7 | If he should do so, he should be banished [as] "taker of upadā".

04.04.9 | The secret agent should say to the grāma-kūṭa or to a superintendent: "That wretch has abundant wealth; this is his loss; therefore have him bring [it]."
04.04.10 | If he should do so, he should be banished [as] "utkocaka".
04.04.11 | Or one under a feigned charge should set about the false witnesses with the known abundance of loss.
04.04.12 | If they should do so, they should be banished [as] "false witnesses".
04.04.13 | By that, the makers of false announcements are explained.
04.04.14 | Or whomever he may think to be one who brings about winning-over (saṃvadana) by mantras, contrivances, root-actions or by cremation-ground [rites], to him the secret agent should say: "I desire the wife, the daughter-in-law or the daughter of so-and-so; let her desire me in return, and let this wealth be accepted."
04.04.15 | If he should do so, he should be banished [as] "one who brings about winning-over" (saṃvadana-kāraka).
04.04.16 | By that, the two [kinds of person] whose practice is kṛtya and abhicāra are explained.
04.04.17 | Or whomever he may think to be a maker, buyer or seller of poison, or one dealing in medicines and food, or a poisoner (rasada), to him the secret agent should say: "That one is my enemy; let injury to him be done, and let this wealth be accepted."
04.04.18 | If he should do so, he should be banished [as] "poisoner" (rasada).
04.04.19 | By that, the dealer in madana compounds is explained.
04.04.20 | Or whomever he may think to be a maker of false coins, a frequent buyer of various metals and alkalis and of charcoal, ash, asandaṃśa, muṣṭika, anvils, bimba, ṭaṅka and crucibles, bearing the mark of hands and clothes smeared with soot, ash and smoke, in contact with a smith's implements, him the secret agent, having entered upon him by pupilhood and by dealings, should make known.
04.04.21 | Once made known, he should be banished [as] "maker of false coins".
04.04.22 | By that, the remover of rāga (colour) and the dealer in false gold are explained.

04.06.3 | As for seizure by the object: one should report the lost and stolen [article], which is not to be found, to the dealers of that kind.
04.06.4 | If, having come upon that [article] which was reported, they should conceal it, they would incur the fault of rendering assistance (sācivya).
04.06.5 | Not knowing, they would be freed by handing over this article.
04.06.6 | And without informing the superintendent of the saṃsthā, they should not make a pledge or a sale of old wares.
04.06.7 | If that reported [article] should be found, he should ask the one seized with the object about its source: “From where was it obtained by you?”
04.06.8 | If he should say: “Obtained from inheritance; obtained from so-and-so; bought; caused to be made; pledged or concealed; this is the place and time of the receiving of it; this is its price, measure, mark and value” — then, when his source is established, he would be freed.
04.06.9 | If the loser should identify that very [article], then of him whose enjoyment [of it] is earlier and long, or whose place is pure, one should know: “The article is his.”
04.06.10 | For even among quadrupeds and bipeds there is similarity of form and mark; how much more [so] with raw materials, ornaments and wares produced from material of one origin by one maker.
04.06.11 | If he should say: “Borrowed; bought; pledged; a deposit; an upanidhi; or service work of so-and-so” — then, by connecting [it] with his apasāra, he would be freed.
04.06.12 | Or if the apasāra should say, “Not so,” the one seized with the object should bring together the reason of the other's giving and of his own receiving, or the indication, through givers, those who cause the giving, recorders, receivers, observers or hearers.
04.06.13 | For one who has found [something] abandoned, lost or fallen, there is clearing (śuddhi) through the indication of place, time and acquisition.
04.06.14 | One not cleared should pay that and as much [again] as a fine.
04.06.15 | Otherwise he would incur the punishment for theft. — Such is seizure by the object.

04.13.1 | For one who makes a Brahmin swallow what is undrinkable or inedible, the highest penalty; [for doing so to] a Kshatriya, the middle; a Vaishya, the first fine for rashness; a Shudra, a penalty of fifty-four paṇas.
04.13.2 | Those who swallow [it] themselves are to be made without a territory (nirviṣaya).
04.13.3 | On going to another's house, by day the first fine for rashness, by night the middle.
04.13.4 | For one who enters armed, by day or by night, the highest penalty.
04.13.5 | A mendicant and a trader (vaidehaka), a drunk and a mad person, [those acting] by force, in adversity, those very close, and those whose entry is established are not to be punished, except [where there is] a prohibition.
04.13.6 | For one who climbs over the enclosure of his own house after midnight, the first fine for rashness; [for one who climbs over the enclosure] of another's house, the middle; and [for one] who breaks a village, garden, fence.
04.13.7 | Caravan-members, with their valuables known, should stay within the villages.
04.13.8 | The village lord should give [for] what of these is robbed and made to go away, not gone out, at night.
04.13.9 | Or, [for] what is robbed, made to go away in the intervals between villages, the superintendent of pastures should give.
04.13.10 | Of the non-pasture [tracts] — the cora-rajjuka.
04.13.11 | Even so, for the unprotected [places] they should make a search by closing off the boundary.
04.13.12 | When there is no boundary-blocking, [it is] a group of five villages or a group of ten villages.
04.13.13 | Having made a weak house, an unpropped cart, a raised post, an unsupported weapon, or an uncovered pit, well or trap-fall, in a case of injury one should know [it as] physical injury (daṇḍa-pāruṣya).
04.13.14 | In cutting a tree, in taking away the reins of a tameable [animal], in the use of an untamed one among quadrupeds, or in driving, in the throwing of wood, clod, stone, stick, arrow, arm, in a vehicle and in a collision with an elephant, one who cries out “Move away!” is not to be punished.
04.13.15 | One killed by an enraged elephant — [one] should give a droṇa of food, a jar of liquor, garland and ointment, tooth-cleaning [materials] and a cloth.
04.13.16 | “Killing by an elephant is equal to the concluding bath (avabhṛtha) of the horse sacrifice” — the washing of the feet.
04.13.17 | In the killing of an uninvolved person, the highest penalty for the driver.
04.13.18 | For an owner who does not release [one] being hurt by a horned or fanged [animal], the first fine for rashness; for [one] who has been cried out to, double.
04.13.19 | For one who has a horned and a fanged [animal] kill each other, the penalty is that and so much.
04.13.20 | For one who makes a god's animal, a bull, an ox or a young cow carry [a load], a penalty of five hundred; for one who sends [it] away, the highest.
04.13.21 | For small animals useful for wool, milk, carrying and breeding: in non-giving, the penalty is that and so much, and in sending away; except for the tasks of the gods and ancestors.
04.13.22 | In an injury [caused by a vehicle] with a cut nose-rope, a broken yoke, one come athwart or face to face, or backing up, or fitted with a wheel, or in a crush of animals and men, the driver is not to be punished.
04.13.23 | Otherwise, in injury to a human being, [one] should impose the penalty as stated.
04.13.24 | In the killing of a non-human creature, the giving of a creature as well.
04.13.25 | When the driver is a child, the owner who is in the vehicle is to be punished; when there is no owner, the one in the vehicle; or the driver who has attained legal capacity.
04.13.26 | The king should take a vehicle occupied by a child or without a man.
04.13.27 | By kṛtya and abhicāra (sorcery and hostile rites), whatever one makes another undergo, that [he himself] is to be made to undergo.
04.13.28 | Granted: the making of winning-over (saṃvadana) — where a wife or maiden is unwilling — [by] one who seeks a wife, or [by] a wife towards a husband.
04.13.29 | In injury done otherwise, the middle fine for rashness.
04.13.30 | For one who has intercourse with the sister of [his] mother or father, a maternal uncle's wife, a teacher's wife, a daughter-in-law, a daughter or a sister: the cutting off of the three liṅgas, and killing.
04.13.31 | A willing woman should receive that very [penalty]; and one enjoyed by a slave, servant, pledged person (āhitaka).
04.13.32 | In the case of an unguarded Brahmin woman: of a Kshatriya, the highest; of a Vaishya, all [his] property; a Shudra should be burnt with a fire of mats.
04.13.33 | In all cases of intercourse with the king's wife, the kumbhī-pāka.
04.13.34 | In intercourse with a śvapāka woman, [the man], marked with the sign of a headless trunk (kabandha), should go to another territory; or a Shudra [should go to] the state of a śvapāka.
04.13.35 | For a śvapāka's intercourse with an Arya woman, killing; for the woman, the cutting off of ears and nose.
04.13.36 | For intercourse with a female renunciant (pravrajitā), a penalty of twenty-four paṇas.
04.13.37 | A willing woman should receive that very [penalty].
04.13.38 | For the forcible enjoyment of a courtesan, a penalty of twelve paṇas.
04.13.39 | For many who have intercourse with one [woman], a penalty of twenty-four paṇas separately.
04.13.40 | For one who has intercourse with a woman in a non-yoni (ayoni), the first fine for rashness; and for one who urinates upon a man.
04.13.41a | For copulation with animals (tiryag-yoni, 'animal wombs'), of one not self-possessed, twelve paṇas; and for intercourse with images of deities, double is held.
