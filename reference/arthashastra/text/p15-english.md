15.01.1 | The livelihood of men is artha (wealth); [it is] the earth inhabited by men — such is the meaning.
15.01.2 | The treatise [that is] the means of acquiring and protecting that earth [is called] the Arthaśāstra.
15.01.3 | It is endowed with thirty-two devices (yukti) — adhikaraṇa, vidhāna, yoga, padārtha, hetvartha, uddeśa, nirdeśa, upadeśa, apadeśa, atideśa, pradeśa, upamāna, arthāpatti, saṃśaya, prasaṅga, viparyaya, vākyaśeṣa, anumata, vyākhyāna, nirvacana, nidarśana, apavarga, svasaṃjñā, pūrvapakṣa, uttarapakṣa, ekānta, anāgatāvekṣaṇa, atikrāntāvekṣaṇa, niyoga, vikalpa, samuccaya, ūhya.
15.01.4 | The matter with reference to which [something] is said is the adhikaraṇa.
15.01.5 | “Having, for the most part, collected those treatises on artha, as many as have been propounded by earlier teachers for the acquisition and protection of the earth, this one Arthaśāstra has been made.”
15.01.6 | The sequence of the sections of a treatise is the vidhāna.
15.01.7 | “Enumeration of the sciences, association with the aged, conquest of the senses, origin of ministers” — [the sequence] beginning thus.
15.01.8 | The joining of sentences is the yoga.
15.01.9 | “The world has four varṇas and āśramas.”
15.01.10 | The padārtha (meaning of a word) is that which has the word as its limit.
15.01.11 | ‘mūla-hara’ is a word.
15.01.12 | “He who unjustly consumes the wealth of [his] father and grandfather is a mūla-hara” — such is the meaning.
15.01.13 | A reason that accomplishes the matter is the hetv-artha.
15.01.14 | “For dharma and kāma (desire) have artha as their root.”
15.01.15 | A concise statement is the uddeśa.
15.01.16 | “The conquest of the senses is the cause of learning and discipline.”
15.01.17 | A detailed statement is the nirdeśa.
15.01.18 | “The conquest of the senses is the not going astray of the organs of ear, skin, eye, tongue and nose in respect of sound, touch, form, taste and smell.”
15.01.19 | “[One] should act thus” — this is the upadeśa.
15.01.20 | “[One] should pursue desire (kāma) without conflict with dharma and artha; [one] should not be without happiness.”
15.01.21 | “Thus that one has said” — this is the apadeśa.
15.01.22 | “[One] should form a council of counsellors [consisting of] twelve ministers,” say the Manavas; “sixteen,” say the Barhaspatyas; “twenty,” say the Ausanasas; “according to capacity,” says Kautilya.
15.01.23 | Establishing by what has been stated is the atideśa.
15.01.24 | “The non-giving of what was given has been explained by ṛṇa-ādāna (the taking of debt).”
15.01.25 | Establishing by what is to be stated is the pradeśa.
15.01.26 | “Or by conciliation, gifts, dissension [and] force, as we shall explain in adversities.”
15.01.27 | Establishing the unseen by the seen is the upamāna.
15.01.28 | “[One] should favour, like a father, those whose exemptions have ended.”
15.01.29 | That which is unstated and comes about from the sense is the arthāpatti.
15.01.30 | “One who knows the ways of the world should resort to a king endowed with self, resources and constituent elements, by way of the agreeable and beneficial.”
15.01.31 | “[One] should not resort by way of the disagreeable and beneficial” — [this] is obtained from the sense.
15.01.32 | A matter having a reason on both sides is the saṃśaya (doubt).
15.01.33 | “[A king] whose constituent elements are impoverished and greedy, or whose constituent elements have been wronged.”
15.01.34 | A matter that is common with another section is the prasaṅga.
15.01.35 | “‘On the land assigned for the work of farming’ — this is the same as the former.”
15.01.36 | Establishing by the reverse is the viparyaya.
15.01.37 | “The opposite [holds] for the dissatisfied.”
15.01.38 | That by which a sentence is completed is the remainder of the sentence (vākya-śeṣa).
15.01.39 | “Like one whose wings are cut, and the king's loss of movement.”
15.01.40 | There “of a bird” is the remainder of the sentence.
15.01.41 | Another's statement that is not rejected is approved (anumata).
15.01.42 | “Two wings, the centre, the reserve: thus [is] the division of the array of the school of Usanas.”
15.01.43 | Description of excess (atiśaya) is explanation (vyākhyāna).
15.01.44 | “And especially of saṅghas, of those following the dharma of a saṅgha, and of royal houses, [there is] dissension caused by gambling, [and] destruction caused by that; thus favouring of the bad is the worst of the vices (vyasana), owing to weakness of the administration.”
15.01.45 | Derivation of a word from quality is etymology (nirvacana).
15.01.46 | “It throws him away from the better; thus [it is] vyasana.”
15.01.47 | An example, one joined with an example, is illustration (nidarśana).
15.01.48 | “For one who is at war with a greater one takes on, as it were, a fight on foot with an elephant.”
15.01.49 | Drawing away of the overrun is apavarga.
15.01.50 | “One should always settle the near army of the enemy, except for suspicion of internal revolt.”
15.01.51 | A word not agreed by others is an own designation (sva-sañjñā).
15.01.52 | “The first constituent element; of him the one next in land [is] the second, the one with one land between [is] the third.”
15.01.53 | A statement to be rejected is the pūrva-pakṣa (the first side).
15.01.54 | “Of the calamities of the king and the minister, the calamity of the minister is more serious.”
15.01.55 | The statement deciding that is the uttara-pakṣa (the later side).
15.01.56 | “Because of dependence on that; for the king stands in the place of the peak of that.”
15.01.57 | [That which is] dependent everywhere is ekānta (lit. 'one-ended').
15.01.58 | “Therefore one should make one's own exertion.”
15.01.59 | “Afterwards it is thus laid down”: [this is] looking to what has not yet come (anāgata-avekṣaṇa).
15.01.60 | “We shall state balance and weights in [the section on] the pautava superintendent.”
15.01.61 | “Before, it is thus laid down”: [this is] looking to what has gone by (atikrānta-avekṣaṇa).
15.01.62 | “The excellence of the minister has been stated before.”
15.01.63 | “Thus, not otherwise”: this [is] niyoga (command).
15.01.64 | “Therefore one should advise him [what is] in accordance with dharma and profitable, and not [what is] contrary to dharma and unprofitable.”
15.01.65 | “By this or by this”: [this is] option (vikalpa).
15.01.66 | “Or daughters born in the most righteous marriages.”
15.01.67 | “By this and by this”: [this is] combination (samuccaya).
15.01.68 | “The self-born is heir of the father and of the kinsmen.”
15.01.69 | The doing of what is unsaid is ūhya (to be inferred).
15.01.70 | “In such a way that the giver and the receiver may not be harmed, so should the skilled arrange the anuśaya.”
15.01.71a | Thus this treatise, furnished with these tantra-yuktis (methods of the treatise), has been stated for the acquisition and the protection of this world and the other.
