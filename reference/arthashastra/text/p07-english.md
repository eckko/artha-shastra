07.01.1 | The circle of constituent elements is the source of the sixfold policy.
07.01.2 | “Peace, war, staying quiet, marching, seeking shelter and the dual policy are the sixfold policy,” say the teachers.
07.01.3 | “The twofold policy,” says Vatavyadhi.
07.01.4 | “For the sixfold policy is accomplished by peace and war.”
07.01.5 | “This is indeed the sixfold policy, by difference of state,” says Kautilya.
07.01.6 | Among these, the pledge-binding is peace.
07.01.7 | Injury is war.
07.01.8 | Neglect is staying quiet.
07.01.9 | Heaping up is marching.
07.01.10 | Giving over to another is seeking shelter.
07.01.11 | The taking up of peace and war is the dual policy.
07.01.12 | Such [are] the six guṇas.
07.01.13 | [One] being diminished by the other should make peace.
07.01.14 | [One] being heaped up should wage war.
07.01.15 | “The other is not able to injure me, nor am I able to injure the other,” [one] should stay quiet.
07.01.16 | [One] endowed with an excess of qualities should march.
07.01.17 | [One] lacking power should seek shelter.
07.01.18 | In a task to be accomplished with help, [one] should resort to the dual policy.
07.01.19 | Such is the establishing of the guṇas.
07.01.20 | Of these, or in whichever guṇa [one] stands, [one] should see: “Standing here, I shall be able to set in motion my own works — the work of forts and embankments, trade routes, settlements on empty land, mines, produce forests and elephant forests — and to injure these works of the other,” [and] should adopt that [guṇa].
07.01.21 | That is growth.
07.01.22 | “My growth will be quicker, greater or of greater yield of growth; the other's, the reverse,” knowing [this], [one] should disregard the other's growth.
07.01.23 | Or, in a growth having equal time, fruit and rise, [one] should resort to peace.
07.01.24 | Or, in whichever guṇa [one] stands [and] should see injury to one's own works, not the other's, in that [one] should not stand.
07.01.25 | This is decline.
07.01.26 | “I shall decline after a longer time, less, or with a greater yield of growth; the other, the reverse,” knowing [this], [one] should disregard the decline.
07.01.27 | Or, in a decline having equal time, fruit and rise, [one] should resort to peace.
07.01.28 | Or, in whichever guṇa [one] stands [and] does not see growth or decline of one's own works, this is stagnation.
07.01.29 | “I shall stay [for a] shorter [time] or with a greater yield of growth; the other, the reverse,” knowing [this], [one] should disregard stagnation.
07.01.30 | “Or, in a stagnation having equal time, fruit and rise, [one] should resort to peace,” say the teachers.
07.01.31 | “This is not explained,” says Kautilya.
07.01.32a | Or if [one] should see: “Standing in peace, with my own works of great fruit I shall injure the other's works; or I shall enjoy my own works of great fruit, or the other's works; by trust in peace or by contrivance, upaniṣad and agents, I shall injure the other's works; or easily, with favour, exemption and ease, through abundance of fruit and gain of my own works, I shall cause the people bringing the yoga of the other's works to flow in — or the other, allied with an exceedingly strong [one], will obtain injury to his own works; or I shall make long the war of him with whom, attacked by me, he makes peace; or [he] will oppress the country of my hater, allied with me — or his country, injured by another, will come to me, then I shall obtain growth in works; or the other, whose undertaking of works has failed [and] who stands in difficulty, might not venture against me in works — or I, whose undertaking of works has started from the other side, allied with those two, shall obtain growth in works; or, having made peace with the enemy, I shall break the circle bound by the enemy — [and] shall obtain the broken one; or, having supported the enemy by favour of the army, in the desire for the circle I shall cause hatred to be taken; the hated one I shall have killed by that very one,” [one] should adopt growth by peace.

07.02.1 | In peace and war, when growth is equal, [one] should adopt peace.
07.02.2 | For in war there are loss, expenditure, absence from home and obstacles.
07.02.3 | By that, among staying quiet and marching, staying quiet is explained.
07.02.4 | Among the dual policy and seeking shelter, [one] should adopt the dual policy.
07.02.5 | For one who has become dual, having his own action as chief, does good only to himself, but one who has taken shelter does good to another, not to himself.
07.02.6 | Of whatever force the neighbouring chief [is], [one] should resort to [one] of force superior to that.
07.02.7 | In the absence of [one of] force superior to that, having resorted to that very [one], [he] should try, unseen, to do good to him by any one of treasury, army and land.
07.02.8 | For coming together with superior force [is] a great fault for kings, except [one] at war with an enemy.
07.02.9 | When [it is] impossible, [one] should behave like one submitting with his army.
07.02.10 | And when [he] may see his life-taking disease, internal revolt, growth of an enemy, calamity of an ally, or [any such] arisen, and growth of himself caused by that, then [he] should withdraw on the pretext of a plausible illness, dharma and business.
07.02.11 | Or, standing in [his] own territory, [he] should not approach [him].
07.02.12 | Or, [being] near, [he] should strike at his weak points.
07.02.13 | Or, gone to the middle of two stronger [ones], [he] should resort to [one] capable of protection, or [one] whose antardhi there may be, or both.
07.02.14 | [He] should remain a kapāla-saṃśraya, pointing out the other to the other as one who takes away the base.
07.02.15 | Or [he] should employ dissension of both, with mutual pointing-out; of the two who have split, secret punishment.
07.02.16 | Or, standing at the side of two established in strength, [he] should counteract from imminent fear.
07.02.17 | Or, having a fort as refuge, having become dual, [he] should remain.
07.02.18 | Or [he] should act by the causes of the sequence of peace and war.
07.02.19 | [He] should support the treasonable, enemies and forest chiefs of both.
07.02.20 | Going to one of these two, [he] should strike, by those very [ones], in the calamity of the other.
07.02.21 | Or, harmed by two, [he] should remain having the circle as refuge; [he] should take shelter with the middle king or the neutral king.
07.02.22 | Together with him, having supported one, [he] should cut off the other, or both.
07.02.23 | Or, cut off by two, [he] should resort to the just conduct of the middle king and the neutral king, or of kings belonging to their party.
07.02.24 | Or, among equals, [the one] whose constituent elements would find pleasure in him; or, standing where, [he] could rescue himself; or where [there is] a course fit for ancestors, or a near connection, [or where] allies would be more numerous or very powerful.
07.02.25a | Dear to whom [he] would be, or who is dear to him: which of the two? [He who is] dear to whom, he should go to him. Such is the highest course of refuge.

07.03.1 | The conqueror, having regard to power, should employ the sixfold policy.
07.03.2 | [One] should make peace with an equal and a superior; [one] should make war with an inferior.
07.03.3 | For, made war upon by a superior, [one] goes, as it were, to a foot-fight with an elephant.
07.03.4 | And with an equal, like an unbaked vessel struck by an unbaked [one], [it] brings about loss on both sides.
07.03.5 | Like a stone with a pot, [one] obtains one-sided success with an inferior.
07.03.6 | If the superior does not wish for peace, [one] should adopt the conduct of one submitting with his army, or the ābalīyasa yoga.
07.03.7 | If an equal does not wish for peace, as much as he would harm, that much [one] should harm him in return.
07.03.8 | For spirit [is] the cause of joining.
07.03.9 | Unheated iron does not join with iron.
07.03.10 | If the inferior should remain submissive everywhere, [one] should accept peace.
07.03.11 | For, like a forest fire, [he] makes the spirit born of distress and indignation advance.
07.03.12 | And [he] becomes one to be favoured by the circle.
07.03.13 | If [he is] allied [and] should see: “The other's constituent elements, [being] greedy, impoverished, ill-treated, or from fear of retaking, do not come over”, even [if] inferior [he] should make war.
07.03.14 | If made war upon [and] [he] should see: “The other's constituent elements, [being] greedy, impoverished, ill-treated, or distressed by war, do not come to me”, even [if] superior [he] should make peace, or should allay the distress from war.
07.03.15 | Even in the simultaneity of calamities, [if] [he] should see: “I am one with a heavy calamity; the other, with a light calamity, having easily remedied his own calamity, might attack”, even [if] superior [he] should make peace.
07.03.16 | If in peace and war [one] does not see weakening of the other or increase of himself, even [if] superior [he] should stay quiet.
07.03.17 | If [one] should see the calamity of the other [to be] irremediable, even [if] inferior [he] should march against [him].
07.03.18 | Or even a superior, [one] whose calamity [is] irremediable [and] imminent, should seek shelter.
07.03.19 | If [one] should see success of the task by peace on one side [and] by war on the other, even [if] superior [he] should remain one who has become dual.
07.03.20 | In this way [is] the employment of the sixfold policy of the equal.
07.03.21 | But there [is] a particular distinction.

07.04.1 | Of peace and war, staying quiet (āsana) and marching (yāna) are explained.
07.04.2 | Staying (sthāna), staying quiet (āsana) and neglect (upekṣaṇa): such are the synonyms of āsana.
07.04.3 | But the difference: staying (sthāna) [is] in a part of the qualities; staying quiet (āsana) [is] for the sake of obtaining one's own growth; non-use of the means [is] neglect (upekṣaṇa).
07.04.4 | For an enemy and a conqueror who desire to outwit [each other], unable to destroy [each other], [there is] staying quiet after making war, or after making peace.
07.04.5 | Or when he sees, "With my own armies, or with the armies of the ally and the forest tribes, I am able to weaken [one] equal or stronger," then, having done the external and internal tasks, he should stay quiet after making war.
07.04.6 | Or when he sees, "My constituent elements, endowed with energy, united, greatly grown, will carry out their own actions unobstructed, or will destroy the actions of the other," then he should stay quiet after making war.
07.04.7a | Or when he sees: "Constituent elements wronged by the other, impoverished, greedy, or harassed by [his] own troops, thieves and forest tribes will come to me of themselves or by secret instigation (upajāpa); my economic activity (vārttā) [is] prosperous, the other's ruined; his constituent elements, struck by famine, will come to me; my vārttā [is] ruined, the other's prosperous, my constituent elements will not go to him; and having made war I shall carry off his grain, cattle and money, or I shall turn back the other's wares that damage my own wares; or, when war is made, [goods] of value from the other's merchant-route will come to me, not to the other; or the one made war upon will not [effect] restraint of the treasonable, enemies and forest tribes, or he will get war with those very [people]; my ally, of friendly disposition, having marched against [him], will obtain wealth with small loss and expenditure in much-little time, or land possessed of good qualities, acceptable; or, desirous of marching with [his] whole gathering, having disregarded me, how should he not march?" — for the sake of warding off the other's growth and for the sake of splendour (pratāpa), he should stay quiet after making war.

07.05.1 | In the equal calamity of the neighbouring chiefs, ‘the one to be marched against, or the enemy?’ — [one] should march against the enemy; on the success of that, [against] the one to be marched against.
07.05.2 | For, on the success of the enemy, the one to be marched against may give assistance; not the enemy, on the success of the one to be marched against.
07.05.3 | ‘The one to be marched against who has a heavy calamity, or the enemy who has a light calamity?’ — “[One] should march against the one with a heavy calamity, from ease,” say the teachers.
07.05.4 | “No,” says Kautilya.
07.05.5 | [One] should march against the enemy who has a light calamity.
07.05.6 | For even a light calamity becomes grievous to one who is attacked.
07.05.7 | Truly, even a heavy [calamity] becomes heavier.
07.05.8 | But the enemy who is not attacked and has a light calamity, having easily remedied the calamity, may advance against the one to be marched against, or may seize the heel.
07.05.9 | In the simultaneity of those to be marched against: ‘the one with a heavy calamity [and] of just conduct, the one with a light calamity [and] of unjust conduct, or the one with disaffected constituent elements?’ — [one] should march against the one with disaffected constituent elements.
07.05.10 | The constituent elements support the one with a heavy calamity [and] of just conduct who is attacked; they neglect the one with a light calamity [and] of unjust conduct; the disaffected [ones] cut off even the strong one.
07.05.11 | Therefore [one] should march only against the one with disaffected constituent elements.
07.05.12 | “‘The one with diminished and greedy constituent elements, or the one with wronged constituent elements?’ — [one] should march against the one with diminished and greedy constituent elements; for diminished and greedy constituent elements easily come to secret instigation or affliction; not the wronged ones — [they are] to be accomplished by restraining the chiefs,” say the teachers.
07.05.13 | “No,” says Kautilya.
07.05.14 | For diminished and greedy constituent elements, attached to the master, stand in the master's welfare, or they frustrate the secret instigation; in attachment [there is] possession of all good qualities.
07.05.15 | Therefore [one] should march only against the one with wronged constituent elements.
07.05.16 | ‘The strong one of unjust conduct, or the weak one of just conduct?’ — [one] should march against the strong one of unjust conduct.
07.05.17 | The constituent elements do not support the strong one of unjust conduct who is attacked; they make [him] fall out, or they resort to his enemy.
07.05.18 | But the constituent elements embrace the weak one of just conduct who is attacked, or go out after [him].
